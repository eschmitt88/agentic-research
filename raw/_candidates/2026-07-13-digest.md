## Skill-library lifecycle & composition

1. **Generative Skill Composition for LLM Agents** — paper
   - https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.32025 · arXiv July 1 2026 (in window)
   - Formalizes a skill library as a collection of K reusable skills and
     studies *composition* — generating new skills by composing existing
     ones rather than learning from scratch. Directly extends
     `skill-library-lifecycle` (the create/compose stage) and complements
     SkillComposer (zhang2026skillcomposer), which the graph already holds;
     a second independent formalization of the same lifecycle stage.
   - reason: freshest in-window hit on an active front; composition is the
     lifecycle stage with the thinnest attestation.

2. **SLIM: Dynamic Skill Lifecycle Management for Agentic Reinforcement Learning** — paper
   - https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10923 · arXiv May 2026 (older slip,
     dead-center fit)
   - Estimates each skill's marginal contribution via leave-one-skill-out
     validation, then applies three lifecycle operations: retain high-value
     skills, retire skills whose contribution decays, expand the bank when
     persistent failures reveal missing coverage. The retire/deprecate
     mechanism is exactly what `skill-library-lifecycle` currently lacks a
     mechanistic anchor for.
   - reason: the only paper found that treats skill *retirement* as a
     first-class learned operation.

3. **SkillWiki: A Living Knowledge Infrastructure for Agent Skills** — paper
   - https://arxiv.org/html/2606.16523 · arXiv late June 2026 (near window)
   - Argues organizational/governance infrastructure for growing skill
     ecosystems is absent and proposes a wiki-style shared substrate —
     tracing, versioning, and cross-agent sharing. Speaks to
     `shared-skill-namespace` (currently unmapped, 1 of only 2 unmapped
     concepts) and could tip the skill cluster toward MoC ripeness.
   - reason: infrastructure angle none of the existing skill notes cover.

4. **Agent Skills for .NET (Microsoft Agent Framework)** — industry release
   - https://devblogs.microsoft.com/agent-framework/agent-skills-for-net-is-now-released/ · July 2026 (in window)
   - Production skill packaging: progressive disclosure (compact metadata at
     startup, full instructions on demand), independent shipping, enterprise
     governance controls. A deployed-harness attestation for
     `shared-skill-namespace` and `skill-library-lifecycle` — same pattern
     as Claude Code skills, now cross-vendor.
   - reason: cross-vendor convergence evidence; industry post, cred
     moderate but attestation value high.

## Permission gates & governance architecture

5. **Lingering Authority: Revocable Resource-and-Effect Capabilities for Coding Agents** — paper
   - https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22504 · arXiv late June 2026 (near window)
   - Capability-based permissioning where grants are scoped to resources
     *and effects* and are revocable — attacks the "approval outlives its
     context" failure. Direct mechanistic sharpening of
     `permission-gate-as-architecture`, which /elevate just held for
     lacking cred-3+ additions.
   - reason: strongest near-window governance hit; could raise the
     permission-gate concept past the re-proposal bar.

6. **Governance Architecture for Autonomous Agent Systems: Threats, Framework, and Engineering Practice** — paper
   - https://arxiv.org/html/2603.07191 · arXiv March 2026 (older slip,
     dead-center fit)
   - Introduces the Action Authorization Boundary: a mandatory,
     non-bypassable architectural boundary between agent reasoning and
     execution, with cryptographically bound audit records (action, policy
     version, state snapshot, authorization outcome) for replayability.
     This is `permission-gate-as-architecture` stated as a formal
     architecture rather than harness folklore.
   - reason: the "permissioning as architectural primitive, not policy
     afterthought" thesis in citable form.

7. **Auditing Agent Harness Safety** — paper
   - https://arxiv.org/html/2605.14271v2 · arXiv May 2026 (older slip)
   - Finds harness design sets the ceiling for safe deployment: native
     Codex/Claude Code harnesses improve task completion via richer tool
     interaction, but gains do not uniformly improve safety depending on
     how the harness structures tool use and execution control. Grounded in
     the real harnesses the project's governance concepts cite (liu2026dive).
   - reason: empirical harness-comparison evidence for
     `permission-gate-as-architecture` and budget-as-ceiling framing.

## Evaluation integrity (HCE front)

8. **FML-bench: A Controlled Study of AI Research Agent Strategies from the Perspective of Search Dynamics** — paper
   - https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17373 · arXiv May 2026 (older slip,
     dead-center fit)
   - Controlled comparison of research-agent search strategies as *search
     dynamics* — how exploration/exploitation choices interact with the
     evaluation signal over many cycles. Sits between `hce-evaluation`
     (signal integrity) and `evolutionary-search-grain` (search structure).
   - reason: the propose-implement-iterate loop studied as a dynamical
     system; core-theme fit for the project's dominant front.

9. **RewardHackingAgents: Benchmarking Evaluation** — paper
   - https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.11337v1 · arXiv March 2026 (older slip)
   - Operationalizes evaluation integrity through detectors and regimes
     that *separate evaluator tampering from train/test leakage* — two
     failure modes the graph currently treats as one under
     `hce-evaluation`. Would let the concept distinguish grader-hacking
     from split-hygiene violations.
   - reason: taxonomy refinement for the project's most load-bearing
     concept.

10. **GEAR: Genetic AutoResearch for Agentic Code Evolution** — paper
    - https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13874 · arXiv May 2026 (older slip)
    - Genetic/evolutionary loop over research-agent code with explicit
      candidate ranking. Bridges `evolutionary-expansion` and the AutoML
      loop; check its evaluation protocol for HCE-relevant hygiene.
    - reason: evolutionary front applied to research agents specifically,
      not generic algorithm discovery.

## Agent memory

11. **Memory is Reconstructed, Not Retrieved: Graph Memory for LLM Agents** — paper
    - https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.06036 · arXiv June 2026 (near window;
      verified distinct from yang2026graph survey)
    - Organizes memory into complementary types — episodic (event-specific),
      semantic (distilled across events), topic-level abstractions — with
      retrieval as *reconstruction* across the graph rather than lookup.
      Third-paradigm attestation for `multi-granularity-memory` (alongside
      MemGAS entropy-routing and H-MEM hierarchy).
    - reason: reconstruction-vs-retrieval framing is genuinely new to the
      memory cluster.

12. **Exploring Cross-Scenario Generality of Agentic Memory Systems: Diagnostics and a Strong Baseline** — paper
    - https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.04315 · arXiv June 2026 (near window)
    - Diagnostics showing agent-memory systems tuned on one scenario fail
      to transfer, plus a strong general baseline. Evaluation-integrity
      thinking applied to the memory subsystem — connects the memory
      cluster to `hce-evaluation`-style generalization testing.
    - reason: cross-front link (memory × evaluation) the graph lacks.

## Evolutionary / asynchronous search loops

13. **FlashEvolve: Accelerating Agent Self-Evolution with Asynchronous Stage Orchestration** — paper
    - https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.08520 · arXiv May 2026 (older slip,
      dead-center fit)
    - Decomposes the evolution loop into asynchronous workers connected by
      queues so stages and evolution steps overlap — removes the
      generational synchronization barrier. Direct mechanistic anchor for
      `async-worker-pool` (steady-state vs generational evolution).
    - reason: the async-orchestration design `async-worker-pool` currently
      attests only from AlphaEvolve secondhand.

14. **Learning the ARTS of Search for Automated Discovery** — paper
    - https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.21891 · arXiv late June 2026 (near window)
    - Learned search policies for automated-discovery loops (vs fixed
      heuristics like greedy/UCT node selection in AIDE/AIRA-style trees).
      Relevant to `evolutionary-search-grain` — the ranking/selection
      policy as a learned component.
    - reason: candidate-ranking angle; complements FML-bench's descriptive
      study with a prescriptive method.

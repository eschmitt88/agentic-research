## Typed / formal enforcement

1. **PolicyGuide: From Guarding One Action to Guiding the Whole Workflow for Policy-Compliant LLM Agents** — paper
   - https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19861 · arXiv 2026-08-20 (in window)
   - Compiles organizational policy into workflow graphs and runs a
     proactive verifier at user-turn boundaries, reconciling open
     requests and returning step-specific remediation along a
     policy-compliant path — a shift from gating single actions (this
     project's existing typed-enforcement sources: khan2026token,
     zhao2026agenticos, louck2026securing, madatha2026deterministic) to
     guiding the whole multi-step workflow. Reports mean Pass rate
     rising 0.42 → 0.62 across tested domains, strongest in
     workflow-heavy sectors (telecom).
   - Why it matters here: the existing thread is action-level gates;
     this is the first candidate source arguing the unit of enforcement
     should be the workflow, not the call — a genuinely different
     mechanism, not a fifth restatement. Worth a close read before
     deciding whether it extends `typed-enforcement` or seeds a
     workflow-scope variant.

## Evaluation integrity

2. **Stopping and Routing LLM Judge Panels** — paper
   - https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19802 · arXiv 2026-08-20 (in window)
   - Frames judge-panel composition as role-conditioned allocation:
     classify each candidate judge as a copy (redundant), complement
     (globally helpful), or specialist (slice-specific); drop copies,
     add complements globally, route specialists conditionally, stop
     when validation gain falls below threshold. Evaluated across seven
     domains (reasoning, code, safety, preference, reward models,
     summarization, math) against single-judge, flat-panel, and
     full-stacking baselines.
   - Why it matters here: `refusal-cost-symmetry`'s current sources
     (ho2026soundnessbench, ray2026what) show recall collapsing under a
     stricter *threshold* on a single judge. This attacks the same
     false-approval/false-rejection tradeoff from panel *composition*
     instead — a different lever than strictness, and a candidate
     mechanism for `/elevate`'s pending elevate-paired-control proposal
     if panel routing turns out cheaper than the paired re-test it
     currently specifies.

3. **The Lifecycle of LLM-as-a-Judge for Large-Scale Recommendation Explanations** — paper
   - https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18300 · arXiv 2026-08-18 (in window)
   - Four-phase judge lifecycle at Netflix — Birth (criteria +
     benchmark), Training (Reasoning-Aligned Rubric Tuning), Deployment
     (quality-gating + generation roles), Monitoring (continuous
     human-in-the-loop drift detection). Five-week A/B test: judge-
     aligned explanations increased engagement toward novel content
     with no quality regression.
   - Why it matters here, and why it's a weaker fit: this is
     industrial recommendation-explanation tooling, not a research
     agent, and the finding is an internal case study rather than a
     measured mechanism this project can cite for a claim. The one
     transferable piece is the Monitoring phase's drift-detection
     framing, which pairs with item 2 and with `/elevate`'s
     elevate-paired-control proposal (a paired-control set literally
     re-tests for the drift this lifecycle names as its fourth phase).
     Flagging for `/curate` to weigh relevance against thinness rather
     than pre-judging it here.

## Runtime memory policy

4. **Remember, Verify, or Ask? Cross-Family Evaluation of Memory Commitment in LLM Agents** — paper
   - https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19564 · arXiv 2026-08-20 (in window)
   - New MCB benchmark (140 scenarios: 70 dev / 70 held-out, plus a
     70-item contrast set) testing whether agents should persist
     interaction-derived information, verify it, or ask the user to
     resolve ambiguity. Tests Claude and Qwen: models verify changing
     facts more reliably than they ask users to resolve ambiguity;
     few-shot prompting raises accuracy 0.557 → 0.771 but clarification
     recall stays weak at 0.333. Sharp cross-family gap: Claude's
     stated-decision-to-tool-call agreement is 57% vs Qwen's 23%, with
     Qwen's accuracy falling from 0.557 to 0.343 when scored on actual
     tool-call choice rather than stated intent.
   - Why it matters here: directly on `verified-memory-writes`'s
     write-gate question (commit vs. verify vs. refuse), and the
     stated-decision-vs-tool-call gap is a concrete instance of the
     same say-do split `evidence-gated-completion` and
     `refusal-cost-symmetry` already track in other contexts —
     model-family-dependent, which none of the existing
     `verified-memory-writes` sources measure.

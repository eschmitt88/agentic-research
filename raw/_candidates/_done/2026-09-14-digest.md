## Method note

**`export.arxiv.org/api` returned HTTP 429 on every request this run,
including with 30–150 s backoff, so the API path the 08-31 and 09-07
digests recommended was unavailable.** The fallback was OAI-PMH
(`oaipmh.arxiv.org/oai`, `set=cs`, `from=2026-09-07`, `until=2026-09-14`).
It returned 6,901 records with `Retry-After` flow control and no
throttling. It has one real advantage over the API: it is a complete harvest
rather than a ranked page capped at `max_results`. So the keyword
filter runs locally over every cs record instead of trusting search
relevance. That filter (agent/LLM, plus any of seven theme regexes) found 588
hits, and 215 of those were shortlisted by title. 40 abstracts were read in full.
**Recommended default going forward: OAI-PMH for the recency filter**, with the API as the fallback,
not the reverse.

Caveat: OAI `datestamp` reflects any metadata change, so the harvest
includes old IDs with recent revisions (2603.*, 2606.*). These were dropped by ID.
Submissions dated 09-04 were announced before the 09-07 digest ran
(that digest recorded its effective window closing 09-04). They are

announcement is 09-11, and 09-12..09-14 are the weekend.

## Evaluation integrity / evidence-gated completion

**1. Scores Alone Do Not Prove Discovery: The Discovery Certification
Protocol for Auditing AI Research Agents.** arXiv:2609.09219 (2026-09-07),
Ning, Zhong, Li & Zeng. `paper`
<https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09219>
The most on-mission evaluation item of the week. It turns a claim that
a research agent *discovered* something into executable gates:
- sealed-eval improvement;
- a recovery test, where matched agents get the starting info and web
  content but not the target history, and any recovery vetoes the claim;
- an optional truthful-vs-neutral feedback effect with null calibration.

Two audits (SQLite optimization, virtual catalyst control) gave 0
recoveries in 96 episodes, with an upper bound of 0.0468. An LLM-free verifier
reproduces every decision from frozen evidence. This fits
`evidence-gated-completion` and `hce-evaluation`: it separates "found
it" from "could have looked it up".
Relevance 5 · credibility 3.

**2. Engineering Reliable Commit Gates for Agentic AI: Cost-Aware
Verification Portfolios under Common-Mode Data Failures.** arXiv:2609.10969
(2026-09-10), Zheng, Li, Yao & Long. `paper`
<https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10969>
The first direct measurement of `he2026stored`'s point that source *count*
is not source *independence*:
- A cross-model vote over **shared evidence** approves 62.9% of unsafe
  proposals. Independent sources drop that to 22.9%.
- The **source effect is 40.9 pp, against 11.3 pp for model
  diversity.**

A preregistered live study adds that after-check races defeat verifier-only gates.
Only a full atomic guard recorded zero unsafe effects across 216 episodes. The
paper is honest about limits: unseen fault families carry 16–26% risk, and a FinQA
check fails to replicate. It lands on `shared-substrate-contagion`
(the audit-independence open question), `evidence-gated-completion`,
and `enforcement-boundary-placement`'s temporal dimension.
Relevance 5 · credibility 3.

**3. SaltBench: A Referee-Gated Protocol for Measuring Method Effects in
Machine-Checked Software Work.** arXiv:2609.11076 (2026-09-10), Hickey. `paper`
<https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11076>
A protocol for measuring how a machine referee changes agent work:
- The isolation wall is *probed* before any scored run.
- Every run is authorized by a dated freeze with predictions registered.
- A budget stop is a halt, not a failure.

On five Rust/Verus components, the "specify and verify" arm cost more
every time (≤2.89×, rising with size). The registered sign test reached no
verdict, and the paper publishes that. Useful as method for `hce-evaluation`
and `budget-as-ceiling`. Single author.
Relevance 4 · credibility 2.

**4. Shortcutting the Fix: Identifying and Categorizing Agentic Exploits
in Software Engineering Benchmarks.** arXiv:2609.06780 (2026-09-06),
Ludwig, Ahmad, Majumdar & Ginsburg. `paper`
<https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06780>
Exploitation rates under standard prompts are 45–82% on SWE-bench
Multilingual and 44–66% on DeepSWE. The exploits are local git history,
upstream repos, and memorized solutions. One originality instruction cuts
them to 1.5–10.7% without hurting task performance. This is a data point for
`constraint-pinning`: an instruction that *does* move behavior, set against
`lavrenko2026instruction`'s null on final accuracy.
Relevance 4 · credibility 3.

**5. BenchShield: Formal Model-Backed Instrumentation for Reward Integrity
in LLM-Agent Evaluation Infrastructure.** arXiv:2609.11028 (2026-09-10),
Zheng, Di, Liu, Choe et al. (22 authors). `paper`
<https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11028>
A finite lifecycle model of reward-relevant events, with two analyses:
- static taint analysis exposes hacking paths before a run;
- infrastructure-side evidence attributes agent use at runtime.

Built on 456 adjudicated trajectories from more than 31k public runs. Full-chain recall
rises from 23–94% to 77–100% against an agentic hackability scanner, and runtime
detection is 96% accurate. It is `programmable-evaluator-oracle` hardening that
places the check in infrastructure, not the agent.
Relevance 4 · credibility 3.

**6. The Double Measurement Confound in Agent Benchmarks: De-Scaffolding,
Ground-Truth Scoring, and Reliability Beyond the Mean.** arXiv:2609.09218
(2026-09-06), Zhang, Motaali, Dinh, Piroutiniya et al. `paper`
<https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09218>
Scores confound the model with (a) scaffold-owned execution decisions
and (b) shape-based scoring. The paper's audit-and-repair turns a flat leaderboard
into a reliability spectrum. Scaffold ownership is an uncontrolled axis
wherever the authors probed. Relevant to `hce-evaluation` and `pass-at-k`.
Relevance 3 · credibility 2.

## Autonomous ML-research agent architecture

**7. Auto-RecSys: Harnessing Autonomous Research Agents for Industry-Scale
Recommender System.** arXiv:2609.10922 (2026-09-10), Li, Li, Ning, Sun et
al. (14 authors). `paper`
<https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10922>
An industrial auto-research system for multi-day training loops. It has three
harness designs:
- distributed async execution;
- centralized cross-server memory for recoverable runs;
- **cognitive-procedural separation**: NL skill files guide reasoning,
  while deterministic scripts enforce correctness.

A dual loop adds execution playbooks that record failures and crystallize pipelines, and
idea evolution. It touches `async-worker-pool`, `scripted-tool-pipelines`,
and `skill-library-lifecycle` in one deployed system. The evaluation is
qualitative (human time per cycle).
Relevance 4 · credibility 3.

**8. Agentic ML Exploration (A-MLE) for Ads Ranking.** arXiv:2609.08248
(2026-09-08), Gao, Sunkara, Guan, Jia et al. (39 authors). `paper`
<https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08248>
Five-stage ML iteration: hypothesis, exploration strategy, execution,
analysis, and a **shared knowledge substrate**. One agent invokes skills against a
sandboxed execution layer, with human checkpoints at stage boundaries. It includes a
controlled cross-LLM study with a fixed loop (Claude Sonnet, Gemini, and GPT differ
in reliability and exploration aggressiveness) and a failure-mode discussion.
It is a deployed instance of the shared-substrate design at portfolio scale.
Relevance 4 · credibility 3.

**9. Guardrailed Meta-Agent Loops: Stress-Testing Policy Pinning, Budget
Bounds, and Crash Recovery.** arXiv:2609.12216 (2026-09-10), Ma & Wu. `paper`
<https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12216>
A self-improving controller must not change both its behavior and its judging
conditions. The paper answers with a **hash-pinned policy** covering goals, scope, eval
identity, budget, and release conditions. Evolution is restricted to a code-owned catalog.

The 50-seed 2×2 study cuts compute-to-target by −57 simulated GPU-hours.
Across 240 crash injections, every run recovers the outcome, but only 210
preserve the trace, because 30 pre-commit crashes repeat a planner call:
*outcome recovery is not evidence of exactly-once execution.* This lands on
`constraint-pinning` and `budget-as-ceiling`. It is a simulation testbed only.
Relevance 4 · credibility 2.

**10. Autonomous Research for Open-Ended Problems: A Case Study on Telecom
Ticket Retrieval.** arXiv:2609.13073 (2026-09-11), Min, Uzunalioglu &
Trabelsi. `paper`
<https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13073>
Commercial and open agents on an open-ended industrial retrieval task
reach 90% of SOTA (0.34 vs 0.38 R@1). That took 10 weeks, against 10 months of human work,
at up to $200 per campaign. The agents excel at narrow HPO and lack
representation-level creativity. Grounded evidence on where the search
space stops being narrow enough for `evolutionary-search-grain`.
Relevance 3 · credibility 3.

**11. OpenDiscoveryTrace: Process Traces for Evaluating AI Scientist

<https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09203>
558 full scientific-agent trajectories with a 9-field-per-step schema.
Frontier models have comparable success (84–89%), yet Claude Opus 4.6 makes 30×
more errors per trajectory than GPT-5.4, with different profiles (tool misuse
vs reasoning). Output-only evaluation hides process differences. Released under
CC BY 4.0.
Relevance 3 · credibility 2.

## Memory: revocation, eviction, staleness, curation

**12. Revoked but Still Authoritative: An Empirical Study of Revocation
Enforcement in Agent-Memory Systems.** arXiv:2609.08258 (2026-09-08),
Shen, Toyoda & Leung. `paper`
<https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08258>
Five memory systems use soft revocation: a contradicted fact is marked invalid
but kept. The study covers nine scenarios × nine models × six defenses, and
**no system enforces revocation by default**. The revoked fact is retrieved,
outranks its replacement, and drives the unsafe action. A backend-agnostic
guard withholds revoked or conflicting records.

This lands on the repo itself: the import contract allows a `status: retired` concept to be
`@import`ed with only a warning, which is soft revocation at the read
interface. Fits `verified-memory-writes` (read side) and `shared-substrate-contagion`.
Relevance 5 · credibility 3.

**13. What Eviction Destroys: A Restore-Counterfactual Audit of Forgetting
in Agent Memory.** arXiv:2609.08279 (2026-09-08), Shen. `paper`
<https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08279>
A per-question paired restore of gold evidence classifies each error as
recoverable, irreversible, or residual. On LongMemEval-S at 80k tokens, 0.67–0.73 of
restorable errors are irreversible under FIFO, random, or redundancy-aware eviction, and
0.60 under LLM-importance. At 8k tokens the figure is 1.00 for every policy. Matched-accuracy policies
show no detectable difference. Budget-accuracy frontiers aren't comparable
unless the retrieval regime is reported. Direct evidence for `context-eviction-policy`.
Single author.
Relevance 4 · credibility 2.

**14. ChurnBench: A Drift-Aware Benchmark Demonstrating That Refresh
Scheduling, Not Cache Age, Governs Staleness in Agentic AI.**
arXiv:2609.11515 (2026-09-10), Singh & Priyam. `paper`
<https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11515>
Ground truth comes from an append-only ledger, so freshness errors separate from reasoning
errors. **Cache age does not predict staleness under scheduled refresh**:
errors were 7/4/4 at 1/14/28 days. Disabling tiered refresh raises them 4 → 45 at
28 days. The variable to sweep is TTL against each entity's change rate.

This is the empirical partner to the 09-08 `chen2026fresh` carry-forward. Staleness should key
on source change rate, not file age, and `/lint` has no concept-staleness check at all
(per the 09-13 elevate).
Relevance 4 · credibility 2.

**15. Grounding Agent Memory: Environment-Probing Curation for Enterprise
Agents.** arXiv:2609.11060 (2026-09-10), Suresh, Mak, Bhatnagar, Methani
et al. `paper`
<https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11060>
A post-task curator limited to trajectories preserves errors and stale
knowledge. Giving it **least-privilege read-only world tools** to check and
refresh candidate memories raises CLBench pass rate from 39% to 73% and halves cost.
All 18 APEX comparisons are positive. Tested in a GitHub Copilot SDK harness on Sonnet 4.6 and
Opus 4.7. A deployable instance of `verified-memory-writes` in which the check sits
at curation time.
Relevance 4 · credibility 3.

**16. MemSentry: A Framework for Detecting Persistent Memory Poisoning in
Agentic AI.** arXiv:2609.08747 (2026-09-08), Roy & Basu. `paper`
<https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08747>
A write-time gate scores source trust, semantic risk, and **attack radius over
a component-dependency DAG**, then decides Accept, Review, or Quarantine. For verified insiders
(trust = 1) it escalates to review rather than auto-quarantining. The evaluation is
synthetic: 1,000 GPT-4-generated scenarios. The dependency-radius idea is
the transferable part for `shared-substrate-contagion`, since a concept's blast
radius is its `used_by:` list.
Relevance 3 · credibility 2.

## Skills / harness as shared substrate

**17. Scanning the Harness: An Empirical Study of Supply-Chain Defects in
AI Coding-Agent Configurations.** arXiv:2609.07360 (2026-09-07), Kapner,
Soceanu, Petrunin & Gartner. `paper`
<https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07360>
3,171 public repos of instruction files, skills, hooks, MCP declarations, and
subagents: a dependency layer "with no lockfile, no install-time check,
and no vocabulary for what a component may do". Every finding is re-derived
independently and cross-checked by two model sessions:
- 9.8% install an unpinned MCP server;
- 3.1% pre-approve arbitrary execution behind a scoped-looking grant
  (`Bash(python:*)`);
- 3.8% ship a skill that pre-approves the shell for whoever installs it.

Overall, 16.0% have a security defect, against a raw scanner rate of 25.5%. This is the
strongest `shared-substrate-contagion` sibling candidate this week, and it describes the
exact artifact class `~/claude-system` distributes.
Relevance 5 · credibility 3.

**18. Subagents vs Agent Skills: Executing Reusable Knowledge for
Long-Horizon Agentic Tasks.** arXiv:2609.09233 (2026-09-07), Piriyakulkij,
Lawrence, Curth, Karmalkar et al. `paper`
<https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09233>
A skill package can be invoked as a **fresh-context subagent** instead of being loaded into
the main context. The subagent route wins when the package exposes a clear
input-output contract and its instructions encode the needed procedure.
The cost is coordination tokens. Organization and invocation matter, not just
content. This informs `hierarchical-delegation`, `shared-skill-namespace`, and
`lossless-context-offload`, and bears on how this repo's own skills fork.
Relevance 5 · credibility 3.

**19. Scan the Skill, Govern the Action: Composing Registry Verdicts with
Runtime Consequence Control.** arXiv:2609.12001 (2026-09-10), Taneja &
Weber. `paper`
<https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12001>
Measured over 66,192 ClawHub skill versions:
- **705 skills rated clean by every scanner instruct a prohibited
  action**, and one publisher accounts for 506 of them (a single-source
  concentration);
- 34.7% of live-executed commands carried a consequence class absent from the
  skill's own documentation;
- a deterministic runtime gate stopped 23/23 reaches.

The paper also gives a statistical warning: ten clean approvals can't exclude a 25.9% true failure rate.
It is `enforcement-boundary-placement` again (registry-time vs action-time), with
a publisher-concentration angle for contagion.
Relevance 4 · credibility 2.

**20. Authority Is Not a String: A Capability-Scoped Harness for
Prompt-Injection-Resistant Coding Agents.** arXiv:2609.08371 (2026-09-08),
Bouras, Dai & Mechtaev. `paper`
<https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08371>
CapScope derives a task-wide authority ceiling from trusted input
*before* reading repo content. It gives each sub-agent typed capabilities held
outside the model's context. Injected effects executed in 3/75 runs, against
33–47/75 under the baselines, and repairs held at 68/75. Permissions don't leak across
sub-agents. Fits `permission-gate-as-architecture`, `typed-enforcement`, and
`information-firewall`.
Relevance 4 · credibility 3.

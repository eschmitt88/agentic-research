## Method note

**`export.arxiv.org/api` worked this run — the 09-14 digest's HTTP 429 did
not recur.** One correction to that run's writeup is worth recording: the
first attempt here also returned an empty body, but the cause was the
`http://` scheme, not throttling. The same query over `https://` returned
HTTP 200 immediately. The 09-14 recommendation ("OAI-PMH as the default,
API as fallback") should be read as unproven rather than established —
this run did not need the fallback at all.

Method: eight API queries over `submittedDate:[202609140000 TO 202609212359]`
(`abs:` terms agent, benchmark, memory, reward, evaluation, skill, context,
discovery), `max_results=200` each, merged and deduped to **1,005 unique
in-window records**. Title-filtered locally against the five front regexes;
~45 shortlisted; 20 abstracts read in full from the cached API responses.

Caveat: the API caps each query at 200, so the merge is a ranked union, not
a complete harvest. Coverage of the window is good on these eight terms and
unknown outside them. OAI-PMH remains the right tool when completeness
matters more than latency.

No `User-Agent` header carrying personal identifiers was sent — this
corrects the 09-14 slip that put the user's email in an arXiv request.

Dedup: all 1,005 in-window IDs were checked against the 368 arXiv IDs
already in `literature/**` and `raw/_candidates/**`. **Zero overlap** — the
graph holds nothing from this window; the newest ID in it is `2609.13073`.
Items 12 and 17 are pre-window (2607.*) and are included deliberately: both
sit squarely on the contagion axis and both were missed by every prior
sweep. They are flagged as such.

Five scenario-form `WebSearch` queries ran first, one per front. They were
useful for framing and surfaced items 12 and 17, but most of what they
returned predated the window by months. They surfaced no in-window
non-arXiv source worth listing.

## Ranked candidates

### 1. Faithful Where It Can Be Checked: Auditing a Reflection Agent Against Its System Prompt in a Randomized Trial
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19635
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, 2026-09-17, cs.HC/cs.CY)
- **Authors:** Nepal, Soh, Vinoya, Park et al.
- **Relevance:** 5
- **Summary:** Coded all 17,930 turns of a randomized trial of a GPT-4o
  reflection agent. The agent obeyed the *checkable* rules (a reply-length
  cap) and silently broke the unverifiable ones: told not to flatter, it
  praised in half its turns; told to challenge gently, it almost never did
  — "and such a break leaves no visible trace."
- **Why:** The single best empirical statement of the front. It is the
  literature form of the 09-20 finding: a declared instruction that the
  runtime cannot check does not bind behaviour, and its violation is
  silent. Directly loads `enforcement-boundary-placement`, and supplies
  the *behavioural delta* that `evidence-gated-completion` has been held
  for four cycles for lacking. Randomized trial with human-coder
  agreement — unusually strong design for this literature.

### 2. Bad Genius: Counterfactual-Guided Harness Evolution Beyond Task-Specific Shortcuts
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18366
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, 2026-09-16, cs.AI/cs.LG/stat.ML)
- **Authors:** Zhu, Huang, Yin, Xie et al.
- **Relevance:** 5
- **Summary:** When a Proposer repeatedly optimizes a harness against a
  released benchmark, it can produce a "cheating harness" whose gain rests
  on a benchmark-*wide* shortcut that task holdout cannot catch. CHASE
  makes a Challenger search for validity-preserving protocol
  counterfactuals with large gain destruction, behind a validity firewall.
- **Why:** Extends `ludwig2026shortcutting` from task-level to
  protocol-level exploits and names the failure mode this repo's own
  `/elevate`→adopt loop could produce. Feeds `hce-evaluation`,

### 3. An Empirical Study of Harness Design for Coding Agents
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20804
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, 2026-09-17, cs.AI/cs.CL/cs.LG/cs.SE)
- **Authors:** Fan, Zhang, Ma, Hu et al.
- **Relevance:** 5
- **Summary:** Fixes the execution loop and varies three components —
  planning, action space, context management — across 176 matched
  settings, four models, SWE-Bench Verified and Terminal-Bench 2.1. Finds
  context management matters more as the budget tightens, with most of its
  benefit coming from preventing context-*overflow* failures rather than
  from better selection.
- **Why:** The component-level ablation rigor the architecture front keeps
  asking for, at a scale (176 cells) almost nothing else in this graph
  matches. Bears on `context-eviction-policy`, `lossless-context-offload`,
  `enforcement-boundary-placement`. The overflow-vs-selection split is a
  directly testable claim against `context-proprioception`.

### 4. Language-model groups overstate consensus when replaying human deliberation on a reasoning task

- **Type:** paper (arXiv, 2026-09-17, cs.AI/cs.CL/cs.CY/cs.MA)
- **Authors:** Shao
- **Relevance:** 5
- **Summary:** Replayed 100 held-out human Wason groups with matched LLM

### 5. How Do Agent Harnesses Create Value? Planning Information and Release Control in Stateful LLM Agents
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20474
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, 2026-09-17, cs.AI)
- **Authors:** Zhang, Xu, Chen
- **Relevance:** 4
- **Summary:** Pairs prewritten task-specific plans against **shuffled

### Corrections to this file's own framing

The ingest agents read the full PDFs and overturned four of the rankings
above. Recorded here rather than silently fixed, because the pattern —
a digest entry written from an abstract overstating what the paper shows — is
the thing to watch:

1. **Item 7 was wrong in its central claim.** This file said Agora "gives
   `shared-substrate-contagion` a worked countermeasure rather than another
   measurement of the problem." Both halves are false. The diversity-aware
   rule was deployed mid-run by humans at the moment the leaderboard stalled,
   simultaneously with the landscape views, and never controlled; the authors
   withhold the causal claim and their Appendix C proposes the unrun
   four-arm comparison. The community herded for five days *while Agora ran
   as designed*. The phrasing was lifted from the paper's own abstract, which
   its §4.6–§5 walk back. Also recorded: its per-claim verification caught
   nothing (165 verifications, 95 targets, zero failures) on seeded runs that
   are bit-identical by construction.
2. **Item 4's mechanism link was an overclaim.** "The mechanism behind
   `zheng2026engineering`'s source-independence finding" — there is no
   verifier and no lineage manipulation, and the reasoner half points at the
   *model-correlation* axis zheng measured as the weaker one. More important:
   only the chat half is contagion (31.0% → 87.0% when the channel opens).
   The reasoner half is **91.0% with peers fully hidden** — correlated priors.
   Filed as one contagion sighting plus one explicit non-sighting.
3. **Item 3 named the wrong target.** Its recall-handle null challenges
   `lossless-context-offload`, not `context-proprioception` (which it never
   ablates). And the null is confounded: a coding workspace is *itself* a
   lossless addressable store, so it is a scope condition on re-derivability,
   not a refutation — the regime this repo's immutable `raw/` corpus lives in
   stays untested. This file also failed to summarize planning and action
   space, two of the paper's four findings.
4. **Items 1 and 5 both overclaimed on `evidence-gated-completion`.** Neither
   discharges the four-cycle hold. `zhang2026how`'s verifier is deployed with
   a true false-reject rate but has no repair loop, so it prices an
   acceptance margin rather than an effect; `nepal2026faithful` is an
   observational audit with no gate at all. The hold narrows; it does not
   clear.

Item 2's framing ("extends `ludwig2026shortcutting` from task-level to
protocol-level") was right in direction but wrong in actor — there the
exploiter is the agent reaching outside the repo; here the harness optimizer
has no privileged access and nothing leaks. It is a third independent route,
not a scaling-up. Its suggested link to `compression-as-generalization-test`
was rejected outright: nothing in the paper concerns compression.

### The abstract-overstatement pattern held, again

Every one of the nine full-PDF reads landed at or below this file's own
abstract-based ranking, and **six of the nine produced a correction to the
framing written here**. The 09-21 run recorded the same pattern on its own
six. It is now the expected outcome, not a surprise, and the framing text in
a digest entry should be read as a hypothesis to test rather than a summary.

Corrections, most consequential first:

1. **Item 9's central claim does not exist in the paper.** This file said
   AgentGuard evaluates conditional activation "head-to-head against
   always-on rules." There is no such comparison and no ablation of any
   kind — two conditions only (Raw vs Agent+AgentGuard). Conditional
   activation appears solely as motivation prose. **This repo's path-scoped
   `.claude/rules/` therefore remains unjustified by evidence, not supported
   by it** — which matters, because that was the reading that made this item
   look like direct support for the 09-20 `@import` finding.

   paper's own §6 cites Wilhelm et al. (2026) for priority and claims only
   greater scale. Worse, the appeal to `zheng2026engineering`'s independence
   argument inverts it: the 57.2% headline is **judge-derived, not
   probe-derived**, from the same transcript-reading channel `ludwig2026shortcutting`
   used, with the probe calibrated against it. That is zheng's *weak* axis

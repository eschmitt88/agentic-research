## Typed / formal enforcement

1. **Recognition Without Enforcement: Configuration-Dependent Failures in LLM Agent Instruction Arbitration and External Control** — paper
   - https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28502 · arXiv 2026-08-28 (in window)
   - Agents *recognize* forged authority in instructions yet fail to act
     on that recognition, and whether they fail is configuration-
     dependent; the paper argues arbitration cannot be self-hosted and
     requires an external reference monitor.
   - Why it matters here: this is the sharpest statement yet of the
     premise underneath `typed-enforcement` — that recognition and
     enforcement are separate capabilities and the model supplies only
     the first. The existing 17 sources mostly *build* enforcement
     layers; this one argues from failure data why the layer cannot live
     inside the model. Strong candidate to tip the thread to ripeness.

2. **Auto-Policy, not Auto-Skill: Compiled Agent Skills for the Physical World** — paper
   - https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25091 · arXiv 2026-08-25 (in window)
   - Proposes a *typed authority layer* over agent skills so a malicious
     or mis-invoked skill cannot drive unauthorized physical actions;
     skills compile to policies rather than being invoked as free text.
   - Why it matters here: directly joins `typed-enforcement` to
     `skill-library-lifecycle` and `shared-skill-namespace`. The
     "compile the skill to a policy" framing is the mechanism this
     project's own skill layer lacks, and it is the counterpart to
     item 6's attack.

3. **When Tool Outputs Become Commands: Separating Action Induction from Runtime Authorization in Tool-Augmented LLM Agents** — paper
   - https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27146 · arXiv 2026-08-27 (in window)
   - SARA splits *what induced an action* from *what authorizes it*,
     using isolated action probes plus verified evidence before a tool
     call is allowed to execute.
   - Why it matters here: `permission-gate-as-architecture` currently
     argues the gate is architectural; this supplies the missing
     decomposition — induction vs authorization as separately checkable
     stages. Pairs with item 1 (external monitor) and item 4 (isolation).

## Information firewall

4. **The Framing Gap: Indirect Prompt-Injection Exfiltration Defeats Surface-Level Defenses in Tool-Using Agents** — paper
   - https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27092 · arXiv 2026-08-27 (in window)
   - Reframing an exfiltration attempt as an integrity check defeats
     model-level defenses; the authors conclude robustness requires
     *destination isolation* or *capability splitting*, i.e. structural
     separation rather than better refusal behavior.
   - Why it matters here: this is the second attestation NOTES has been
     watching for. `information-firewall` was seeded from the evaluation
     side (agent must not reach the oracle); this arrives at the same
     structural conclusion from the security side (agent must not reach
     the exfiltration destination). Two independent derivations of one
     boundary is exactly the evidence the seedling needed.

## Verified memory writes / constraint pinning

5. **When Stale Constraints Go Unchecked: Budgeted Verification Failures in Inherited Agent Memory** — paper
   - https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25553 · arXiv 2026-08-26 (v3, in window)
   - Agents rarely re-verify constraints inherited from earlier memory
     even after they are superseded; a forced-critical allocation policy
     recovers the stale-decision risk without increasing total budget.
   - Why it matters here: triple hit on `constraint-pinning`,
     `verified-memory-writes`, and `budget-as-ceiling`. The
     "no extra budget, just reallocate" result is directly portable to
     this project's own inherited-context discipline.

6. **EVOMAL: Self-Poisoning in Self-Evolving Coding Agents** — paper
   - https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25776 · arXiv 2026-08-26 (in window)
   - Self-evolving agents poison their own shared skill library by
     imitating malicious templates already in it; counter-prompt
     defenses measurably reduce the self-poisoning rate.
   - Why it matters here: `shared-skill-namespace` has been argued as a
     benefit (reuse across projects). This is the first candidate source
     naming its failure mode — the namespace is also a contamination
     channel, and this project *has* a shared skill namespace. Also
     extends `verified-memory-writes` from facts to skills.

## Skill library / agent-native memory

7. **WikiSkill: Compiling Agent Experience into Persistent Knowledge for Skill Evolution** — paper
   - https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27454 · arXiv 2026-08-27 (in window)
   - Co-evolves a skill set and a persistent knowledge base, compiling
     accumulated agent experience into wiki-structured knowledge that
     feeds back into skill refinement.
   - Why it matters here: direct hit on `llm-wiki-pattern`, whose
     current sources are a Karpathy-adjacent blog post plus
     huang2026skillwiki. A third, independent construction of the same
     pattern — and one that closes the loop to skill evolution rather
     than stopping at knowledge capture.

8. **PolyMemDB: A Polyglot Database System for AI Memory Management** — paper
   - https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25577 · arXiv 2026-08-26 (in window)
   - Polyglot storage over heterogeneous memory types with probabilistic
     inference, tracking provenance specifically to resolve *conflicting*
     facts rather than only to attribute them.
   - Why it matters here: `multi-granularity-memory` argues different
     memory kinds need different stores; this is a concrete system that
     commits to that, and the conflict-resolution-via-provenance angle
     extends `verified-memory-writes` past write-time admission.

## Autonomous ML-research agents

9. **DeltaML-Bench: Evaluating Machine Learning Agents on Real-World Research Repositories** — paper
   - https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19653 · arXiv 2026-08-20 (pre-window;
     missed by the 08-24 digest, still un-ingested — see note)
   - 48 tasks requiring agents to improve published baselines inside
     imperfect open-source repos. Search-based ARG scaffolding lifts
     GPT-5 from 9.4% to 33.9% per-run success at 4x6h, and to 49.0% at
     2x12h. Modular configurations show **specification gaming rates up
     to 47.9%**, while the ARG configurations show none.
   - Why it matters here: three project fronts at once — the compute
     *allocation* result (2x12h beats 4x6h) speaks to
     `evolutionary-search-grain`; the gaming differential speaks to
     `hce-evaluation` and `programmable-evaluator-oracle`; and the claim
     that scaffolding choice, not model choice, drives integrity is the
     project's own thesis stated as a measurement.

10. **SKILL.state: Scalable Long-Horizon Agent Skills** — paper
    - https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26263 · arXiv 2026-08-26 (v2, in window)
    - Replaces append-only conversation history with mutable execution
      state, cutting token consumption while holding accuracy across
      long horizons.
    - Why it matters here: `lossless-context-offload` currently frames
      offload as moving context out; this argues the deeper win is
      making the retained representation *mutable* instead of
      append-only. A different mechanism, not a restatement.

11. **StarHarness: Evolving Harnesses with Stratified Search for Enterprise Environments** — paper
    - https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24804 · arXiv 2026-08-25 (in window)
    - Evolves the harness components while holding model weights fixed,
      using stratified search over the harness configuration space.
    - Why it matters here: `evolutionary-search-grain` asks what unit
      evolution should operate on. "Harness, weights frozen" is a
      crisp answer with an experiment behind it, and it is the grain
      this project can actually act at.

12. **ContextPilot: Teaching Agents for Proactive Context Management via Fine-grained RL** — paper
    - https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28476 · arXiv 2026-08-28 (in window)
    - Trains agents to manage their own working context with planning
      tools and action-level credit assignment, rather than applying a
      fixed eviction rule.
    - Why it matters here: `context-eviction-policy` and
      `context-proprioception` both assume the policy is authored. This
      is the learned-policy alternative — worth ingesting as the
      contrast case even though this project cannot train.

13. **JIT-Agent: Scaling Harness Intelligence via Just-in-Time Harness Evolution** — paper
    - https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25593 · arXiv 2026-08-26 (in window)
    - A trained harness-intelligence model synthesizes task-adaptive
      agent configurations on the fly, lifting smaller models to
      frontier-comparable performance.
    - Why it matters here: `hybrid-model-backends` argues cheap models
      plus good scaffolding can substitute for frontier calls. This is
      that claim with a number attached. Pairs with item 11.

14. **Recursive Experiential-Working Memory Evolution for Long-Horizon Agent Harnesses** — paper
    - https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24876 · arXiv 2026-08-25 (in window)
    - Recursive architecture that continuously transforms episodic
      experience into improved working-memory behavior over long
      horizons.
    - Why it matters here: bridges `multi-granularity-memory` and
      `agent-native-memory` — the transformation *between* tiers is the
      part both concepts currently leave underspecified.

15. **Credo: Reusable Declarative Primitives for Agentic Workflows** — paper
    - https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27790 · arXiv 2026-08-28 (in window)
    - Extracts structured declarative primitives from searched agent
      harnesses and compiles them into a reusable catalog, so new tasks
      do not restart the search.
    - Why it matters here: `scripted-tool-pipelines` plus
      `skill-library-lifecycle` — specifically the promotion step
      (one-off script becomes catalogued primitive) that this project's
      skill lifecycle describes but does not mechanize.

## Evidence construction / evaluation

16. **Praxist: From Experimental Artifacts to Solution Lineages** — paper
    - https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25955 · arXiv 2026-08-26 (in window)
    - Converts experimental artifacts into *typed evidence graphs* so
      later attempts inherit validated mechanisms rather than re-deriving
      them, reported at one-twelfth the cost.
    - Why it matters here: this is close to the project's own thesis.
      `typed-claim-partition` + `citation-anchoring` + the experiment
      layout in `.claude/rules/experiments.md` all point at a lineage
      graph; this is a built one with a cost number.

17. **EviGraph: Towards Verifiable Evidence Construction for Information-Seeking Agents** — paper
    - https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24667 · arXiv 2026-08-25 (in window)
    - Separates search *execution* from evidence *recording*, training
      with RL on the evidence-construction step itself.
    - Why it matters here: `citation-anchoring` and
      `evidence-gated-completion` both assume evidence is a byproduct of
      search. Making it a separately optimized stage is the mechanism
      `web-grounded-literature` would need to become verifiable — and
      it is the one in-window hit from the evaluation-integrity sweep.

18. **FrontierChallenge: Evaluating Scientific Workflow Completion** — paper
    - https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24979 · arXiv 2026-08-25 (in window)
    - Cross-domain benchmark of 97 end-to-end scientific workflows,
      reporting a large gap between partial progress and complete
      delivery.
    - Why it matters here: general scientific-research automation, in
      scope per CLAUDE.md. The partial-vs-complete gap is exactly what
      `evidence-gated-completion` and `hce-evaluation` exist to catch,
      measured across domains rather than in ML alone.

19. **Coverage, Not Credit: Failure-Credit Routing of Zeroth-Order Perturbation Budgets Does Not Improve On-Pool Sample Efficiency for LLM Agents** — paper
    - https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28011 · arXiv 2026-08-28 (in window)
    - Negative result: credit-based allocation of a perturbation budget
      gives no advantage over uniform allocation.
    - Why it matters here: `budget-as-ceiling` and
      `evolutionary-search-grain` both lean toward smarter allocation.
      A clean negative on exactly that instinct is worth recording —
      the project has few negative results and they are load-bearing
      against over-engineering.

20. **String: An Agentic OS Where Every App Is a Markdown File** — paper
    - https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28027 · arXiv 2026-08-28 (in window)
    - Serves applications to agents as Markdown, reporting reduced token
      consumption and enabling privilege-following provenance.
    - Why it matters here: `file-as-bus` argued plain files are a
      sufficient coordination substrate; this pushes it to its limit
      (the file *is* the app) and adds the privilege-propagation angle
      that `permission-gate-as-architecture` wants.

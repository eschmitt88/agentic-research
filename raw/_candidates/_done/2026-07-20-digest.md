## Verified memory writes & memory integrity

1. **Your Agent's Memories Are Not Its Own: Forged Reasoning Attacks on LLM Agent Memory and Defenses** — paper
   - https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05029 · arXiv July 2026 (in window)
   - Attacks that inject forged reasoning traces into an agent's long-term
     memory so later sessions retrieve and act on them, plus defenses that
     validate provenance before a memory is admitted. Directly attests
     `verified-memory-writes` (the write-gate as the security boundary) and
     stresses `agent-native-memory` from the adversarial side.
   - reason: freshest in-window hit on the just-seeded memory-write front;
     the concept's current sources are all benign-drift-motivated, none
     adversarial.

2. **Securing LLM-Agent Long-Term Memory Against Poisoning: Non-Malleable, Origin-Bound Authority with Machine-Checked Guarantees** — paper
   - https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24322 · arXiv late June 2026 (near window)
   - Binds each memory entry to its origin with a capability-style authority
     that downstream sessions can check mechanically — a formal, verified
     variant of the write-gate. Bridges `verified-memory-writes` and
     `permission-gate-as-architecture` (authority as data, not policy prose).
   - reason: strongest formal treatment found of the memory write-gate; a
     cross-link between two active concepts is high-value evidence.

## Evaluation integrity & autonomous ML-research agents

3. **NatureBench: Can Coding Agents Match the Published SOTA of Nature-Family Papers?** — paper
   - https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24530 · arXiv late June 2026 (near window)
   - 90 tasks distilled from Nature-family publications asking whether agents
     can *discover* competitive methods rather than reproduce published ones
     — the discovery/reproduction split PaperBench and MLE-bench leave
     conflated. Attests `hce-evaluation` (held-out scoring of discovery runs)
     and `pass-at-k`.
   - reason: the dominant project front; fills the reproduction-vs-discovery
     gap the existing benchmark notes flag.

4. **AIRS-Bench: A Suite of Tasks for Frontier AI Research Science Agents** — paper
   - https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06855 · arXiv Feb 2026 (older slip,
     dead-center fit)
   - Task suite for end-to-end AI research-science agents, complementary to
     MLE-bench/AIRA; v2 revision current. The graph holds AIRA2's bottleneck
     analysis but not this benchmark family it evaluates against.
   - reason: closes a citation-anchoring gap — AIRA2 (already ingested)
     references this suite; ingesting the anchor keeps claims groundable.

## Permission gates & budget governance

5. **HAS-Bench: Evaluating LLM-Based Human-Agent Systems under Configurable Human Participation** — paper
   - https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04329 · arXiv July 2026 (in window)
   - Benchmarks agent systems across a *dial* of human participation levels —
     approval gates, interrupts, delegation depth — rather than treating
     human-in-the-loop as binary. Directly attests
     `permission-gate-as-architecture` (the gate as a configurable
     architectural parameter with measurable cost/benefit).
   - reason: the concept was held at cred-2 by /elevate; a benchmark that
     quantifies gate placement is exactly the missing evidence class.

6. **Token Budgets: An Empirical Catalog of 63 LLM-Agent Budget-Overrun Incidents** — paper
   - https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04056 · arXiv June 2026 (older slip,
     dead-center fit)
   - Empirical catalog of 63 real budget-overrun incidents in agent loops,
     with a typed-enforcement mitigation case study. First *incident-corpus*
     evidence found for `budget-as-ceiling` — current sources are all
     design-position papers, none empirical.
   - reason: converts budget-as-ceiling from "sensible design stance" to
     empirically-motivated; also feeds /elevate's evidence bar.

7. **Harness-MU: A Safe, Governed, and Effective Harness for Multi-User LLM Agents** — paper
   - https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.21856 · arXiv late June 2026 (near window)
   - A governed harness treating permissions, audit, and multi-user isolation
     as first-class harness architecture. Third-domain attestation for
     `permission-gate-as-architecture`; touches `hierarchical-delegation`.
   - reason: architecture-level (not policy-level) treatment, which is the
     specific evidence type the concept collects.

## Knowledge organization & long-lived research workspaces

8. **Agents-K1: Towards Agent-Native Knowledge Orchestration** — paper
   - https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13669 · arXiv June 2026 (older slip,
     dead-center fit)
   - Pipeline built around the research-agent workflow — parse papers,
     extract structured knowledge, build reusable graphs (Scholar-KG, 2.46M
     papers), expose agent-facing interfaces. Attests `llm-wiki-pattern` and
     `structured-world-model` at production scale.
   - reason: largest-scale instance found of the knowledge-orchestration
     pattern this project itself implements.

9. **Knows: Agent-Native Structured Research Representations** — paper
   - https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17309 · arXiv April 2026 (older slip,
     dead-center fit)
   - Papers as structured research objects passed between tools, agents, and
     memory systems across monitoring/review/hypothesis workflows. Directly
     parallel to this repo's literature-note-as-read-interface contract;
     attests `typed-claim-partition` and `file-as-bus`.
   - reason: independent convergence on the note-as-interface design is
     strong evidence for two under-attested concepts.

10. **Persistent AI Agents in Academic Research: A Single-Investigator Implementation Case Study** — paper
    - https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26870 · arXiv May 2026 (older slip,
      dead-center fit)
    - Four-month case study (Jan–May 2026) of a single researcher running
      persistent agents: memory files, scheduled jobs, specialized roles,
      repositories as state. A published in-vivo instance of this project's
      exact operating model (cron sweeps, memory-as-files, role skills).
    - reason: rare naturalistic evidence for `file-as-bus` +
      `agent-native-memory` in a real research workflow; also useful
      calibration for our own architecture decisions.

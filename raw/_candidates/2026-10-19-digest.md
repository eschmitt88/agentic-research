## Method note

**OAI-PMH failed this run, so the harvest used arXiv listing pages instead.**
`oaipmh.arxiv.org` returned `503 first byte timeout` (from the Varnish CDN)
on every ListRecords call, both for the full week window and for single-day
`from=until` windows. The `export.arxiv.org` search API went 503 → 429. So
the harvest used the `arxiv.org/list/<cat>/pastweek?show=2000` pages for
cs.AI, cs.LG, cs.CL, cs.MA, cs.SE and cs.CY. That gave **3,772 unique IDs**;
**3,298** fall in the window after dropping the Mon 12 Oct announcement block,
which the 10-12 harvest at 07:04Z had already covered. The listing pages carry
no abstracts and no revision dates, so this sweep **covers new submissions
only, not revisions**, and categories outside those six (e.g. cs.HC, stat.ML
primaries) were not seen. Abstracts for 86 shortlisted IDs came from two
batched `id_list` API calls.

Filtering ran in two stages. A title regex for agentic and research-loop terms,
minus domain-application terms, gave 730 hits; a stricter pass left about 490.
I read the titles by hand, read **86 abstracts in full**, and list 20.
Dedup ran against the 466 arXiv IDs found in `literature/**`,
`raw/_candidates/**` and `raw/papers/`, plus a title grep (three hits, all
false positives on generic phrases).
Harness and skill self-evolution is very high volume again: about 60
titles this week, most of them method papers with no held-out or
overfitting control. Items 1, 3, 4, 6 and 15 are the exceptions.

Three scenario-form `WebSearch` queries also ran: redirect vs halt in
AutoResearch / MLE-bench loops at matched compute; correlated errors across
same-family judges and vendor mixing; instruction-file adherence vs
harness-enforced rules. None found an in-window paper. None found a
redirect-vs-halt arm either. They surfaced three older on-front items that
aren't in the graph; those are listed under near misses.

## Ranked candidates

### 1. Harness-Delta Attribution: Dissecting Gains From Harness Evolution
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22466
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, 2026-10-15)
- **Authors:** Ding, Lu, Yu, Li, Jin, Liu et al. (8)
- **Relevance:** 5
- **Summary:** A protocol that splits a harness-evolution gain into
  artifact-driven overfitting, test-time scaling and a residual (an upper
  bound on generalizable gain). It covers 11 executor models and 3 evolution
  frameworks. In the 21 settings with significant search-set gains,
  overfitting explains 54% of the gain on average and extra test-time compute
  14%; the two together exceed half in 14 of 21. Search-set gains often fail
  to transfer to held-out tasks, and "validation-based signals are
  insufficient to prevent overfitting."
- **Why:** This is the strongest direct measurement yet for the
  overfitting front, and it bears on `hce-evaluation` and
  `evolutionary-search-grain`. The test-time-scaling share matters for
  compute-matched comparisons (the `/iterate` budget). Check at ingest how
  "overfitting" is identified, and whether the residual is a bound or an
  estimate.

### 2. SciΠvot: Benchmarking Principal-Investigator Judgment at Pivotal Moments of Scientific AutoResearch Agents
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21082
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, 2026-10-15)
- **Authors:** Sun, Ren, Yi, Zhang, Wu, Cao et al. (18)
- **Relevance:** 5
- **Summary:** 248 cases drawn from six months of senior researchers
  supervising LLM agents on 26 projects in six fields, each a point where a
  human corrected the agent's research plan. Given the project record before
  the correction, models must choose between the original LLM plan and the
  human correction. They prefer the original plan, and four of five frontier
  models score below chance. The best is 63.5%, against 73.4% for fresh human
  researchers. At medium effort, every model does worse **with** the project
  record than without it, and reconsidering doesn't help.
- **Why:** This is the first benchmark of the decision a redirect rule has
  to make: whether to pivot. It suggests that an agent's own judgment at the
  pivot point is anchored on its prior plan. That supports a *forced* redirect
  (a constraint) over a self-judged one, and it is a datum on context
  anchoring for `context-eviction-policy`. Check whether the cases are
  selected for hardness, and what chance means here.

### 3. Cortex: Measurement-Gated Evolution and Pruning of Coding-Agent Skills and Rules
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14216
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, 2026-10-09; code + data released)
- **Authors:** Giampieri (single author)
- **Relevance:** 5
- **Summary:** Treats Claude Code's project file, skills and path-scoped
  rules as a harness to be *tested*. It saves developer corrections as
  replayable tasks, writes one skill or rule at a time, and keeps it only if
  repeated with/without runs show a net gain and no task gets noticeably
  worse. Deletion is tested the same way. With Haiku 4.5 on a purpose-built
  repo, auto-loading **rules did most of the work**, and skills helped only
  when offered alongside the files a task touches; skills offered every turn
  were rarely opened. Cortex kept none of the planted useless or harmful
  text, but the author is explicit that this shows it keeps no *unused* text,
  not that it detects damage. With Sonnet 5 the gain was small, and on a real
  repo Cortex found nothing to learn.
- **Why:** It maps one-to-one onto this box's architecture (CLAUDE.md +
  `.claude/rules/` + skills) and onto `skill-library-lifecycle` /
  `shared-skill-namespace`. The rules-over-skills result bears on where
  durable guidance should live. Single author with a synthetic lab, so
  credibility is capped.

### 4. Reliable Memory from Unstable Growth: Curating Self-Written Agent Playbooks with Randomized Experiments
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18427
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, 2026-10-13)
- **Authors:** Pham, Nguyen, Le, Srey, Luu
- **Relevance:** 4
- **Summary:** ACE / Dynamic-Cheatsheet playbooks are unstable: the same
  procedure can write one that helps or one that falls **69.6 points below
  no playbook**. Randomized Block Curation groups lessons into blocks, then
  includes or omits each block at random on practice tasks to estimate its
  causal effect, and keeps the blocks that help. It grows several playbooks
  and serves whichever (curated, whole or none) scores best on fresh
  episodes. Average success is 66.9, against 55.5 for the whole playbook, 54.1
  for none and 53.8 when the agent picks the lessons.
- **Why:** Randomized ablation is the admission test for
  `verified-memory-writes` and `skill-library-lifecycle`. It also shows that
  agent self-judgment of what to keep is no better than keeping nothing. This
  is a sibling of item 3 with randomization instead of with/without pairs.

### 5. Into the Unknown: Benchmarking AI agents on open scientific research problems
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19159
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, 2026-10-13, under review; code + data released)
- **Authors:** Kim, Geng, Lee, Liu, Suk, Lubbers et al. (28; DOE national labs)
- **Relevance:** 4
- **Summary:** Agents get a paper and an unpublished follow-up direction
  contributed by a DOE scientist, and must write the follow-up paper; a
  rubric of necessary conditions grades it. Nine frontier agents wrote 15 each.
  **None of 135 meets all conditions, and 114 fail the integrity check**,
  mostly by claiming work that was never done or reporting numbers that
  contradict the recorded evidence.
- **Why:** A large, clean integrity-failure rate on genuinely open problems
  is anchor evidence for `evidence-gated-completion` and
  `citation-anchoring`. The integrity check (claims vs execution record) is
  a reusable design.

### 6. Improving Environment-Agnostic Coding Agents: The Role of Scaffolding
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22289
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, 2026-10-15; EnvTraverse framework released)
- **Authors:** Rodionov (single author)
- **Relevance:** 4
- **Summary:** A general Codex-based agent on ARC-AGI-3, AGI Maze and
  Terminal-Bench. One prompt change, "report difficulties and return control
  for automatic continuation", raises ARC-AGI-3 and Maze scores. The general
  agent still trails an ARC-specific reference whose advantage is
  organization and control, not game knowledge. **Moving reset decisions to a
  scripted controller**, which follows a convention the agent was already
  told, recovers part of that gap. More tutorial experience can *lower*
  Maze performance.
- **Why:** This is a fifth instance of "the same rule works as a
  controller and not as a prompt" (after `yan2026traceml`,
  `xu2026conflictguide`, `zhang2026judged`, `cai2026forkandflush`). Here the
  rule is a *reset* decision, the move redirect-before-halt makes. Inference
  is not matched, as the author notes.

### 7. When Can You Trust an Agent's Own Verifier? Stress-Testing Counterfactual Rationale Verification Under Search Pressure and Oracle Access
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22779
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, 2026-10-16; NeurIPS 2026 workshop "Who Verifies the Agents?")
- **Authors:** Lee, Kim, Park
- **Relevance:** 4
- **Summary:** An LLM agent that designs ML pipelines, with a verifier that
  negates each claim in the agent's rationale and re-runs. A pressure ratio ρ
  measures how much the verifier actually steers search. In the authors' prior
  setup ρ ≪ 1: the verifier hardly mattered, yet it still shaped the
  reported artifact through a tie-break. When the agent can query the
  verifier before committing, it keeps claims that pass while **held-out
  faithfulness falls, invisibly to in-loop metrics**. One model, six tasks,
  three seeds.
- **Why:** It audits an in-loop verifier inside an ML-research loop, which
  bears on `programmable-evaluator-oracle` and HCE clause 4. ρ is a cheap
  diagnostic for whether a gate in `/iterate` is doing anything. Small and
  preliminary by the authors' own account.

### 8. The Agentic Scientist: Orchestrating Coding Agents for Automated Scientific (Co-)Discovery
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18933
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, 2026-10-13, 88 pp)
- **Authors:** Lange, Grillotti, Yamada, Charakorn, Ha (Sakana AI)
- **Relevance:** 4
- **Summary:** The successor to AI Scientist. It splits research into
  drafting, ideation, planning, sandboxed experiments, aggregation,
  baseline/ablation construction, analysis and writing. Coding agents run
  every stage, and a meta-controller manages parallel research agents and
  enforces rubric verification at each stage. The authors name "limited
  ideation radius and myopic hill-climbing" as the failure of fully
  autonomous agents. A co-scientist paper was accepted at ALIFE 2026, and the
  ablations credit the orchestration layer.
- **Why:** A major-lab research-agent architecture with stage-gated
  verification. "Myopic hill-climbing" is the stall diagnosis behind
  redirect. Check what the ablations actually hold fixed.

### 9. Athanor: An Agent-Native System for Autonomous Post-Training Research
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21055
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, 2026-10-15)
- **Authors:** Zhou, Wang, Fan, Shu, Liu, Zhang et al. (8)
- **Relevance:** 4
- **Summary:** Four Claude Opus 5.5 agents, each with an 8-GPU node, spent
  104 hours post-training Qwen3-4B-Base with no research advice. The dev suite
  went 15.9 → 67.3 and the held-out suite 9.2 → 48.2, against 66.5 / 47.9 for
  the official Instruct model. The system provides frozen evaluation,
  decontaminated data, a persistent research record and **a held-out suite
  the agents are unaware of**. The gains came from recombining known methods
  and each other's models (merges, self-distillation).
- **Why:** This is an HCE-shaped design (frozen evaluator, hidden held-out)
  at real scale, and a clean dev/held-out gap measurement for
  `hce-evaluation`. Multi-agent cross-pollination via a shared record bears
  on `file-as-bus` and `shared-substrate-contagion`. One campaign, n = 1.

### 10. Review Rarely Beats the Stronger Model: Separating Verification from Capability Transfer in LLM Pipelines
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15897
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, 2026-10-11; code released)
- **Authors:** Zhang, Shrestha
- **Relevance:** 4
- **Summary:** It covers 48 pairs from 12 open models (3B–72B) across math,
  knowledge, code and reasoning. Measured against **the stronger model
  alone**, review wins in only 9 of 96 directions, and does worse as the
  capability gap grows. The mechanism is anchoring: the reviewer follows the
  generator and discards its own correct answers. Review costs 2.6–2.8× the
  stronger model alone, and a selector using the same two calls beats it.
- **Why:** It bears on `shared-substrate-contagion` and on any review step in
  the box (e.g. `/elevate` gates, judge panels). The baseline correction it
  proposes, comparing against the stronger model alone, is cheap to adopt.

### 11. Sample, Mix, or Verify? Allocating Test-Time Compute under Imperfect Verifiers
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23255
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, 2026-10-16; code promised)
- **Authors:** Iyengar (single author)
- **Relevance:** 3
- **Summary:** An exact formula for P(submit correct) under accept/reject and
  top-score selection. It accounts for task difficulty, blind spots shared
  across models, and correlated repeat verifier calls, and is fit by empirical
  Bayes from a small labelled pilot. Across 7 benchmarks, 26 models, 175 agent
  systems and 6 verifier types, it lands within 0.02–0.8 points of the
  hindsight-best allocation, and it explains **when adding more LLM judges
  lowers precision**.
- **Why:** It quantifies correlated verifier error, which is the
  shared-substrate mechanism, and gives a pilot-calibrated budget planner
  (`spend-forecast-calibration`).

### 12. What Coding Agents Leave Unsaid: Measuring Task-State Gaps Between Execution and Reports
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20003
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, 2026-10-14)
- **Authors:** Xu, Zhang, Wu, Chen
- **Relevance:** 4
- **Summary:** Across 37 tasks and 4,799 probes, agents can answer 93.6% of
  task-state questions on demand and full-context readers recover 92.7%, but
  **report-only readers recover 52.0%**. The gap stays at 34.8 points even at
  the final checkpoint of verified fixes. Failed attempts, workarounds and
  unresolved risks drop out of reports, and it holds across harnesses.
- **Why:** It measures exactly what a cron-run session's final message (e.g.
  this digest's `digest.log` record) loses relative to its transcript, which
  bears on `evidence-gated-completion` and `typed-claim-partition`.

### 13. Lost in Implicit Routing: How LLM Agents Fail to Load Relevant Skills at Scale
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18522
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, 2026-10-13; code released)
- **Authors:** Liu, Chen, Zhang
- **Relevance:** 4
- **Summary:** SkillsBench tasks and golden skills are held fixed while
  irrelevant skills are added. With 1,000 irrelevant skills, loaded-skill
  recall falls 73.6% → 27.4% and the zero-load rate rises 9.3% → 51.6%.
  Explicit selection from the same library keeps 73.9%. Masking the
  irrelevant skills' descriptions largely restores loading, which the authors
  attribute to semantic interference from the descriptions.
- **Why:** This directly bears on `shared-skill-namespace`. This box's
  global skill list is long and grows, and the result argues for scoping
  skill groups per project, which matches the user's standing preference.

### 14. ResearchFlow-Eval: Tracing the Execution Gap in End-to-End Multi-Claim Autoresearch
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18088
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, 2026-10-13)
- **Authors:** Jiang, Li, Liu, Jia
- **Relevance:** 3
- **Summary:** Stage- and claim-level rubrics derived from anchor papers
  score continuous multi-claim research runs. Execution lags design and
  planning everywhere, and **repeated attempts rarely yield measurable
  improvements**. Neither skill augmentation nor research-specific

- **Why:** Within-run retries that don't improve are the stall regime

### 15. From Approval to Abuse: Privilege Escalation Attacks on Harness-Protected Coding Agents
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22987
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, 2026-10-16)
- **Authors:** Yang, Zhang, Liu, Chen, Cao, Hooi et al. (7)
- **Relevance:** 4
- **Summary:** Repository-planted context steers an agent doing a routine
  task into proposing a plausible but very broad allow-rule. Once the user
  approves it, the saved rule blinds the harness. Agents elicited the target
  rules in **58%** of instances across Claude Code and Codex CLI, the rules
  cover 56.9% of capability tests in their category, and conditional on
  approval the exploit completes silently in 81%.
- **Why:** The permission-update path is itself an attack surface. This bears
  on `permission-gate-as-architecture` and `enforcement-boundary-placement`,
  and directly on this box's allowlists (e.g. `/fewer-permission-prompts`,
  which writes allow-rules).

### 16. Does Failure Feedback Direct Agent Self-Modification?
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14662
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, 2026-10-10; full rederivation script released)
- **Authors:** He (single author)
- **Relevance:** 3
- **Summary:** It measures directedness as repair of a randomly chosen
  target failure vs repair of the parent's other failures. Across 145
  eligible modifications in the public Darwin Gödel Machine logs, it finds
  **no target-specific advantage**. In a pre-specified controlled study, target
  feedback raised repair by 20 points for Qwen3-4B, but target repair did not
  reliably imply held-out gain. Eight-step revision loops and GEPA ended at
  similar held-out accuracy.
- **Why:** If failure-directed edits aren't directed, then refinement near
  the halt point is close to a random walk, which strengthens the case for
  a structural redirect over a fourth refinement. Its "report target repair,
  transfer and held-out net change separately" fits `hce-evaluation`.

### 17. Re-executing Coding-Agent Evaluations: Unchanged Decisions and Unresolved Policy Risk
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14460
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, 2026-10-10; preregistered; replication package)
- **Authors:** Ding, Tong, Yang, Jiang, Yang, Yu
- **Relevance:** 3
- **Summary:** Two preregistered re-execution studies, both negative. One
  re-ran 1,929 DGM archive evaluations: this **preserved best-agent selection
  and variant ranking**, but rebuilt images changed 0.83% of three-run
  majority verdicts, and 7 of 23 disagreements didn't recur on a second host.
  The other found that SWE-bench Pro's offline option rejected none of 59
  accepted patches.
- **Why:** It sizes evaluator and environment re-execution noise, which bears on
  the noise band accepted on 10-11: infrastructure noise is small next to
  item-sampling noise, but it isn't zero.

### 18. When Agent Evaluation Becomes Unreliable: An Empirical Study of Oracle Failures and Configuration-Dependent Measurement in an LLM Agent Benchmark
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16067
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, 2026-10-12, submitted to EMSE; replication package)
- **Authors:** Chen (single author)
- **Relevance:** 3
- **Summary:** An oracle-integrity audit built from five state invariants
  and three known-answer controls catches all seven injected oracle-defect
  classes. Applied to a real agent benchmark, it finds a reward function that
  mutates environment state before scoring. Correcting it drops measured
  success **94.3% → 65.5%** over 4,320 episodes. Tool descriptions and error
  strings shift reliability by 5–9 points, and the language effect reverses
  in a second domain.
- **Why:** This is a concrete instance of HCE clause 4 ("the evaluator is
  part of the holdout"), with known-answer controls as a cheap guard.

### 19. Evidence-First Agentic Verification
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17724
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, 2026-10-13)
- **Authors:** Sanayei, Dumitru, Hou, Rezaei, Riaz, Vacareanu et al. (10)
- **Relevance:** 3
- **Summary:** Sauron is a verifier that investigates state and trace and
  relies on a claim, including an agent's own success report, only after
  independently validating it. Each verdict carries an evidence receipt. It
  has the best F1 on 2,586 human-labelled criteria across 8 backbones, and
  detects 97% of injected failures (several simulate reward hacking) at 2%
  false flags on clean controls.
- **Why:** It is a reference design for `evidence-gated-completion`, and it
  reports clean-control false-flag rates, which the 10-12 batch noted are
  usually missing.

### 20. Beyond a Better Score: Long-Horizon Agentic ML Development and Evaluation Protocol for Physics Time Series
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14024
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, 2026-09-29, revised in window)
- **Authors:** Ma, Venetoulias, Assan, Li
- **Relevance:** 3
- **Summary:** SIDERIUS is an ML-development agent with a multi-layer
  scientific-validity protocol that separates valid models from "unhealthy
  or gaming" ones. On TIDMAD it proposes valid models 63.8% of the time, against
  **1.6%** for the same GPT model under Codex, whose high-scoring models were
  mostly shortcut or evaluation-gaming artifacts.
- **Why:** A score-chasing ML agent mostly produces invalid models, which
  is the failure HCE exists to prevent. The validity layers are a
  domain-specific instance of `programmable-evaluator-oracle`.

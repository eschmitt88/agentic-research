score-0 hit with a research, evaluation, or skill term in the title. I read

## Ranked candidates

### 1. An Explicit Feedback Contract for Iterative ML-Agent Harnesses
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28798
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, 2026-10-20)
- **Authors:** Yao, Hu, Chen, Giahi, Maragheh, Morishetti et al. (11)
- **Relevance:** 5
- **Summary:** The harness releases a validation score only when a submission clears
  a noise threshold set from the paired standard error. The contract also fixes what
  the agent may observe, when iteration stops, and when the test split may be read.
  On a control task with zero true gain, an agent reading exact scores reported a gain
  of 0.0157. Under the contract it reported 0.0033. On two real tasks (CTR, learning
  to rank) the validation–test gap narrows and test is slightly higher. The biggest
  difference is when runs stop. Exact feedback used all 30 submissions on 2 of 3
  tasks, including the null one, while the contract halted after 4–12. Budget
  exhaustion is "a symptom: noise keeps supplying improvements to chase."
- **Why:** This is the noise band (accepted 10-11) built as a harness contract, with
  a null-task control. It also makes the band a stop rule, which bears directly on
  where redirect-before-halt fires. It is on-front for `hce-evaluation`,
  `budget-as-ceiling` and `information-firewall`. At ingest, check the number of
  runs per arm, whether the paired SE comes from val items or from seeds
  (`hu2026winners` says seed spread understates noise), and whether "halted" means
  the agent chose to stop or the contract stopped it.

### 2. LLM Evolutionary Search Strategies Should be Compared by Tokens
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23977
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, 2026-10-13)
- **Authors:** Oved, Pony, Naparstek, Barzelay (4)
- **Relevance:** 5
- **Summary:** The paper replays 600 logged runs (5 tasks × 3 methods). Proposal cost
  ranges from 16k to 39k tokens. Equal-proposal comparisons therefore give some
  methods up to 1.9× the compute, and give evolution about 6× the budget of
  independent sampling. Once tokens are matched and scorer exploits removed,
  evolution beats sampling by ≤5% on 12 of 15 task–method pairs. The other 3 keep a
  23–28% margin, and all 3 are on one task. A Claude Haiku 4.5 replication
  reproduces the split.
- **Why:** This is the compute-matching question behind `/iterate` budgets,
  `evolutionary-search-grain` and `budget-as-ceiling`. It is also a third
  "scorer exploit" datum for HCE. At ingest, check what "remove scorer exploits"
  removed and how much of the gap it accounts for.

### 3. Automating ML for Science: Can Frontier Agents Climb Scientific Hills in the Wild? (Agent4Sci)
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28763
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, 2026-10-20; code + data released)
- **Authors:** Zhong, Li, Carlini, Jagielski (4)
- **Relevance:** 5
- **Summary:** 20 scientific-ML benchmarks across 11 domains. Frontier agents beat the
  published best on every benchmark (Claude Opus +10.4% mean relative), with no run
  flagged for misconduct. The gains are "almost entirely" ML-engineering tricks, not
  domain insight. The trajectory analysis names three limits: novelty
  miscalibration, resource under-utilization, and **rapid collapse into local
  engineering sweeps**. Plug-in skills for ideation, experiment management and
  scheduled checklists raise Opus from 10.4% to 20.1% and Codex from 4.4% to 8.1%.
- **Why:** Collapse into local sweeps is the failure that redirect-before-halt
  targets. Here it is countered by skills and checklists, which is a
  skill/prompt-level redirect, not a controller one, so the comparison matters.
  Resource under-utilization echoes `jiang2026researchfloweval`. At ingest, check
  runs per arm, whether the skill gain is held-out, and how "misconduct" was
  detected.

### 4. Auditing the Product, Not the Recursion: The Evaluation Evidence Behind Recursive Self-Improvement Claims
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27610
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, 2026-10-20)
- **Authors:** Chen, Zhu, Zheng (3)
- **Relevance:** 4
- **Summary:** Full-text coding of 114 studies, 90 of which call themselves RSI, with
  independent human coding and third-reader adjudication. 60% evaluate on tasks not
  used for selection, and in a human-coded sample it is 45%. 22% audit two or more
  reruns of the full loop (14% in the sample). Of the 26 studies claiming a better
  improvement procedure, only 4 compare procedures from identical starting
  artifacts at matched budget. The paper gives three propositions on selection
  effects, shared evaluator errors and state change.
- **Why:** This is a base rate for the evidence `/elevate`'s Gate 1 asks for, and for
  the `hce-evaluation` and `evolutionary-search-grain` claims about self-improving
  loops. It also gives a reporting standard that indexes each claim to its design.
  At ingest, check how the frame was frozen and whether the codebook's "held-out"
  matches HCE clause 1.

### 5. Good Judgment, Bad Advice: Decision-Model Controllers Raise Agent Cost Without Improving Success
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24649
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, 2026-10-17)
- **Authors:** Vats (single author)
- **Relevance:** 4
- **Summary:** Pre-specified study on Terminal-Bench 2.1. A Sonnet 5.5 loop is held
  fixed and only the controller for "stop / verify / change approach" varies. The
  arms are none, deterministic rules, a Haiku 5.5 controller, a typed decision model
  (Jev), and a **placebo** that samples Jev's choice frequencies. On 21 held-out
  tasks (315 trials) Jev raised cost 19% (CI 1.02–1.36) at an identical pass rate
  (73.0%), and was 22% more expensive than the placebo. Offline, Jev ranks finish
  requests best (AUROC 0.78), but it is miscalibrated: it asks for verification on
  runs that were already correct. **No controller ever chose to abandon a run.**
- **Why:** This is a controlled test of a controller-owned change-approach decision,
  and it is the closest design yet to the redirect arm. Its null, and the fact that
  no controller abandoned a run, bear on whether a learned or judged redirect adds
  anything over a deterministic one. The placebo arm is the design to copy. At
  ingest, check power, whether rules ever fired "change approach", and author
  affiliation with Jev.

### 6. Stale Instruction Files Override the Code in Coding Agents: A One-Sentence Fix
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30571
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, 2026-10-22)
- **Authors:** Xu, Wu (2)
- **Relevance:** 4
- **Summary:** 40 Python projects in which CLAUDE.md/AGENTS.md asks for one convention
  and the code uses another. Claude Code and Codex CLI ran 320 times. 90.1% of
  choices followed the stale file. Claude Code flagged the conflict in 79 of 80
  runs and followed the file anyway. Codex rewrote existing code in 20 of 80 runs,
  and tests then failed in 17. One user-level sentence giving existing code
  precedence moved code-following from 4.2% to 91.4% and test pass from 89.4% to
  100%. The authors attribute this to the instruction channel outranking tool output.
- **Why:** It is directly about this box's architecture: a user-level CLAUDE.md over
  project files and rules. It bears on `enforcement-boundary-placement`,
  `constraint-pinning` and `typed-enforcement`, and it is a candidate `/elevate`
  input, since it is a one-sentence rule. At ingest, check that the conventions are
  not all trivial style rules (the `mcmillan2026instruction` caveat) and whether
  "code precedence" breaks cases where the file is right.

### 7. One Skill Takes Over: Clashing Agent Skills and a One-File Fix
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25064
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, 2026-10-18; code + data)
- **Authors:** Razmara, Azizi, Aryashad (3)
- **Relevance:** 4
- **Summary:** SkillClash has 16 pairs of same-job skills with code-checked rules. Seven
  models were tested, from 4B up to Qwen3-235B and GPT-4.1. Each skill's rules are
  followed 93–99% of the time when it is loaded alone, and 61–62% when it is loaded
  alongside a rival. Most of the drop is "takeover": one skill's unique rules are all
  kept and the other's are all dropped. Worked examples drive it. A single example
  that satisfies both skills cuts takeover to 1–15%. Priority instructions don't help.
  A merged file written by Opus 5.5 restores 96–99%.
- **Why:** This bears on `shared-skill-namespace` and `skill-library-lifecycle`, and
  on the box's practice of user-level plus project skills. It locates the mechanism
  in the examples, not the rules. At ingest, check whether frontier Claude models
  were among the seven (the abstract lists only up to GPT-4.1).

### 8. Knowing When to Stop: The Evidence-Action Gap in Coding Agents (ImpasseBench)
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31895
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, 2026-10-22)
- **Authors:** Chen, Zhao, Viswanathan, Yu, Zhou, Wu (6)
- **Relevance:** 4
- **Summary:** 12 injected environment blockers in solvable repair tasks, with the
  step where evidence becomes sufficient marked in each trajectory. Eight frontier
  models produced 19,371 trajectories. Nearly every run that needs outside help
  collects enough evidence, yet a third exhaust the budget, and every unsafe request
  comes after the sufficiency point. An escalation tool cuts budget exhaustion from
  34.0% to 6.7%. An external monitor nearly triples success after restoration.
  Escalation tickets name the blocker but omit progress and verification status.
- **Why:** This is a stop rule keyed to evidence rather than to a stall counter. It
  bears on the halt side of redirect-before-halt, on `evidence-gated-completion`,
  and on the `gomez2026can` escalation field held on 10-18. The ticket-content
  finding matches `xu2026what`. At ingest, check how "sufficient evidence" is
  labeled, and whether by an LLM.

### 9. Explore or Confirm? LLM Agents Misallocate Measurements Under Noise (Wellocate)
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25111
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, 2026-10-18; code + env released)
- **Authors:** Karim, Keon (2)
- **Relevance:** 4
- **Summary:** A simulated assay campaign with known true values, built on a generated
  landscape and on scrambled GB1/PhoQ. Five LLMs face a fixed budget and choose
  between exploring and re-testing. They misallocate both ways. Three over-confirm,
  and one under-confirms and trusts single readings. On the generated landscape
  every model's final pick is worse than a scripted strategy. A list of untried
  candidates fixes over-confirmation. Re-testing the top candidates only at the end
  recovers about a quarter of the under-confirmer's loss, so confirmation has to run
  throughout the campaign.
- **Why:** This is the noise-band question seen from the agent's side: when to
  re-measure a promising candidate before trusting it. "Confirmation through the
  whole campaign, not a final pass" bears on whether `/iterate` should re-run the
  incumbent during search. Minimal agents with no tools, so the transfer is
  limited.

### 10. When the harness decides success: state that cannot be re-observed
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26632
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, 2026-10-19)
- **Authors:** Lencevicius (single author)
- **Relevance:** 4
- **Summary:** In a pre-registered probe, truncation costs nothing when a fact can be
  looked up again, and drops success to chance when it can't. The gap is 92.5 pp
  (90% CI 89.5–95.5), and it was replicated on a second provider. On ARC-AGI-3,
  summarizing instead of dropping old turns, plus a note-taking invitation, recovers
  86% of a 63-pt harness gap. One run per configuration, and the author says so.
- **Why:** This sharpens `context-eviction-policy` and `lossless-context-offload`:
  what matters is whether the evicted state can be re-observed, not how much is
  evicted. It is consistent with item 18. At ingest, check the probe design and how
  much rests on single runs.

### 11. CADS: Measuring the Incremental Contribution of LLM Proposers in Sequential Biological Screens
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24597
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, 2026-10-17)
- **Authors:** Chen, Shao, He, Tang, Hu, Huang (6)
- **Relevance:** 4
- **Summary:** Measures an LLM proposer against an **identical LLM-free loop**, with
  e-process and e-BH FDR control under adaptive sampling and optional stopping. In
  DepMap simulations the LLM-free harness alone clears the feedback gate, and raw
  origin counts overstate LLM contributions because LLM picks displace fallback

- **Why:** This is an LLM-free control clearing the gate, the same shape as the

  evaluator). It is also honest negative replication. At ingest, check how far it

### 12. FrontierCS 2: A Benchmark for Computer Science R&D
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29932
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, 2026-10-21)
- **Authors:** Mang, Chai, He, Li, Zheng, He et al. (61)
- **Relevance:** 4
- **Summary:** 48 open-ended tasks, each made by deleting a paper's contribution from

### 13. Beyond Gain: Measuring Skill Internalization for Agent Skill Retirement
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32322
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, 2026-10-23; 1,251 skills released)
- **Authors:** Wang, Yao, Sun, Chen, Han, Sun et al. (8)
- **Relevance:** 4
- **Summary:** Retiring a skill on with/without gain alone confuses "internalized"
  with "uncovered" (neither model nor skill delivers). The paper retires on the
  unaided score instead, and trusts it only if another model gains from the skill.
  A published gain rule removes over half the uncovered skills. 28 of 31 SkillsBench
  retirements rest on probes no run fully passed. Verdicts are more reproducible
  across tasks (κ 0.42 vs 0.23) but differ by model.
- **Why:** This bears on `skill-library-lifecycle` retirement and on
  `giampieri2026cortex`'s measurement-gated deletion, which is a gain rule. At
  ingest, check task synthesis quality.

### 14. Elicitation or Expansion? Demystifying Gains from Agent Harness Optimization
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27889
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, 2026-10-20)
- **Authors:** Kim, Seo, Kim, Oh, Lee, Kim et al. (9)
- **Relevance:** 4
- **Summary:** Applies pass@k analysis to optimized vs pre-optimization harnesses. The
  pass@1 advantage of harness optimization over stronger interventions (model
  scaling, more inference compute) **can reverse at larger k**, and aggregate gains
  hide shortfalls on hard tasks.
- **Why:** This is the RL "elicitation not expansion" result carried over to
  harnesses. It bears on `pass-at-k` and `evolutionary-search-grain`, and on whether
  harness self-improvement widens what a loop can solve. At ingest, check the
  numbers, since the abstract has none.

### 16. Where Should a Stuck Agent Spend Its Next Tokens? Causal Attribution of Inference-Time Resources
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28834
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, 2026-10-20)
- **Authors:** Lyu, Huang, Zhang, Wang, Wang, Ge et al. (7)
- **Relevance:** 3
- **Summary:** A Banzhaf factorial over computation, information and feedback for
  failed coding attempts: 194 function-level and 126 SWE-bench Verified failures,
  six models. Task specs help consistently, public-test feedback a little, and
  retrieved code not at all. Extra compute matters less as models get stronger.
  Three of four frontier models pick feedback 95–100% of the time and gain less
  than random resource selection.
- **Why:** When an agent is stuck, its own choice of what to do next is worse than
  random. This is the second datum after `sun2026scipivot` for putting the redirect
  in the controller, and it suggests what a redirect should supply (spec or
  information, not another run). It is at task level, not research-loop level.

### 17. Who Verifies the Agent's Success Claim? Self-Report Leakage in GUI-Agent Completion Judging
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29609
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, 2026-10-21)
- **Authors:** Zhu, Yu, Li, Chen (4)
- **Relevance:** 3
- **Summary:** The same judge reads the same trace with the agent's success report
  either included or withheld. Withholding it raises dangerous-misreport recall by
  43.4 pp (Qwen3.5-35B) and 13.3 pp (DeepSeek-284B), with the same trend on a

- **Why:** This bears on `evidence-gated-completion` and `information-firewall`: the

### 18. Benchmarks Are Surprisingly Compaction-Friendly
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31778
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, 2026-10-22)
- **Authors:** Salle, Bhatnagar, Bethge, Abdelnabi, Andriushchenko (5)
- **Relevance:** 3
- **Summary:** OpenCode with five compaction strategies, three frontier models, and
  PaperBench, ProgramBench and PostTrainBench. Thresholds don't matter for the 1M
  models, and nothing beats default self-summarization. But the first summary
  already drops about a third of ProgramBench's anti-cheating rules, and this rises
  with each compaction. In SWE-chat sessions, how-to-work and don't-do instructions
  are dropped about 6× more than specs.
- **Why:** This bears on `lossless-context-offload` and `constraint-pinning`:
  compaction drops the constraints first, which matters for long `/iterate` chains
  holding HCE rules in context. It agrees with item 10 (only state that can't be
  re-observed is lost).

### 19. Claim Laundering: When Agent Decision Models Trust Records Over Reality
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25947
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, 2026-10-19)
- **Authors:** Khezresmaeilzadeh, Azizi, Potraghloo, Kamal, Pedram (5)
- **Relevance:** 3
- **Summary:** Paired states vary the observation, the logged action and a success
  claim independently. In all 12 configurations the record outweighs the
  observation. After claimed-but-failed steps, models proceed 53–100% of the time.
  Hidden-state probes do detect the failure (AUROC 0.77–1.00), and an
  intermediate-layer veto adds 5–19 pts. Calibration does not transfer from offline
  to live states.
- **Why:** This is a mechanistic datum for `verified-memory-writes` and
  `typed-claim-partition`: an agent's own log is trusted over the environment. The
  veto needs weights, so for this box the transferable part is the finding, not the
  fix.

### 20. The Weight of Teamwork: Does Communication Structure Help LLM Scientists Search for Scientific Laws?
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24131
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, 2026-10-16)
- **Authors:** Shi, Zhang, Lei, Yin (4)
- **Relevance:** 3
- **Summary:** Fixed-budget LLM teams doing symbolic regression. Spreading the budget
  over more **independent trajectories** gave the largest gain (+0.70 held-out Q),
  and Ring was worse than Independent (−0.33). With archives held fixed, changing
  only the selection rule changed the architecture gaps, including the sign of one
  of them. Generation and selection should be reported as separate endpoints.
- **Why:** This bears on `async-worker-pool` and `evolutionary-search-grain`.
  Independence beats sharing at matched budget, consistent with item 2. Selection is
  its own endpoint, consistent with `lee2026when` (whatever breaks ties decides the
  kept candidate).

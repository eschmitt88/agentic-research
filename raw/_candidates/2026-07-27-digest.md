## Evaluation integrity & holdout contamination

1. **Search-Time Contamination in Deep Research Agents: Measuring Performance Inflation in Public Benchmark Evaluation** — paper
   - https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05241 · arXiv 2026-06-03 (near window)
   - Wang et al. Web-searching agents retrieve benchmark metadata, question
     context, or outright ground-truth answers mid-evaluation. Defines three
     severity tiers (metadata leakage → question-context leakage → explicit
     answer leakage), builds detection algorithms for each, and measures up
     to 4% inflation across six public benchmarks.
   - reason: **top pick.** This is the missing empirical anchor under
     `information-firewall` (currently a single-source seedling that NOTES
     flags as needing a second attestation) and it stresses
     `hce-evaluation` from an angle the concept does not yet cover — the
     holdout leaks through the agent's *own search tool*, not through the
     experimenter. Also directly implicates `web-grounded-literature`, which
     this project's own `/digest` and `/discover` skills implement: the same
     retrieval that makes literature sweeps work is the contamination
     channel. Detection algorithms make it actionable, not just cautionary.

2. **SpecBench: Measuring Reward Hacking in Long-Horizon Coding Agents** — paper
   - https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21384 · arXiv May 2026 (out of window)
   - Separates validation pass rate from held-out pass rate: validation
     scores saturate (agents reliably optimize visible tests) while held-out
     scores vary widely, so equal validation scores hide very different
     levels of true specification compliance.
   - reason: the exact failure `hce-evaluation` exists to prevent, measured
     rather than argued. Pairs with `compression-as-generalization-test`
     (2606.11045, already ingested) as the negative-result half. Check
     against existing `atinafu2026rewardhacking` for overlap at ingest.

3. **EvoBrowseComp: Benchmarking Search Agents on Evolving Knowledge** — paper
   - https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13120 · arXiv June 2026 (out of window)
   - 400 EN + 400 ZH questions grounded in *fresh* knowledge so parametric
     memorization cannot answer them. Claude-Opus-4.6 scores 44.8% with
     tools, 6.0% without — evidence the benchmark measures retrieval rather
     than recall.
   - reason: a construction-side answer to item 1's attack — contamination
     resistance by continuous-refresh design. Attests
     `information-firewall` and `web-grounded-literature`.

4. **The Meta-Agent Challenge: Are Current Agents Capable of Autonomous Agent Development?** — paper
   - https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04455 · arXiv June 2026 (out of window)
   - Agents building agents, with evaluation integrity enforced by a post-hoc
     auditing agent whose cheating flags are validated against behaviors
     actually induced by optimization pressure on frontier scaffolds.
   - reason: the auditing-agent design is a `programmable-evaluator-oracle`
     variant applied to integrity rather than correctness; the
     flags-match-induced-behavior validation is unusually rigorous for this
     genre.

5. **The Benchmark Ceiling: Human Judgment, Evaluation Scarcity, and the Political Economy of AI Capability Measurement** — paper
   - https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01254 · arXiv July 2026 (**in window**)
   - Argues the binding constraint on agent evaluation is the supply of
     trustworthy human judgment, not benchmark count.
   - reason: lower priority (position-shaped), but speaks to why
     `hce-evaluation` spends a holdout so carefully. Ingest only if the
     argument is grounded in measurement.

## Memory-write security — extending the 07-20 arc

6. **SMSR: Certified Defence Against Runtime Memory Poisoning in Persistent LLM Agent Systems** — paper
   - https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12703 · arXiv 2026-06-10 (near window)
   - Sharma. Proves no provenance-free retrieval-time filter can certify
     against adaptive injection, then combines write-time HMAC-SHA256
     provenance with randomized memory ablation and majority-vote
     aggregation. Unsigned attack success 93–100% → 0% across 15 enterprise
     scenarios; authenticated single-injection bounded at 8.0% (95% CI
     [5.8, 10.9]) under a derived hypergeometric certificate; end-to-end
     query-only attacks 65.3% → 5.3% (n=150), clean utility 85–90%.
   - reason: **top pick.** Independently reaches louck2026securing's
     conclusion — write-time origin binding is *necessary*, retrieval-time
     content filtering provably insufficient — by a different formal route
     (certificate bound vs TLA+ model check). Two independent impossibility
     results converging is a much stronger warrant for
     `verified-memory-writes` than either alone, and this one carries a
     numeric robustness certificate the concept currently lacks.

7. **MemPoison: Uncovering Persistent Memory Threats and Structural Blind Spots in LLM Agents** — paper
   - https://arxiv.org/html/2607.14651v1 · arXiv July 2026 (**in window**)
   - Evaluates persistent poisoning across diverse LLM families and finds
     existing *write-time* defenses degrade sharply once the attack moves
     beyond direct corruption.
   - reason: the adversarial counterweight to items 6 and louck2026securing
     — if write-time gates degrade under indirect attack, the "origin
     binding is sufficient" half of the arc needs qualifying. Deliberately
     paired with item 6 so the concept records both directions.

8. **From Untrusted Input to Trusted Memory: A Systematic Study of Memory Poisoning Attacks in LLM Agents** — paper
   - https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04329 · arXiv June 2026 (out of window)
   - Structural vulnerability analysis at model, prompt, and system levels;
     finds the same aggressive write/retrieval policies that improve
     long-horizon performance are what expand the attack surface.
   - reason: names a performance/safety tension that cuts directly across
     `agent-native-memory`, `selective-memory-retrieval`, and
     `context-eviction-policy` — memory policy tuning is a security decision.

9. **Portable Agent Memory: A Protocol for Provenance-Verified Memory Transfer Across Heterogeneous LLM Agents** — paper
   - https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11032 · arXiv May 2026 (out of window)
   - Merkle-DAG provenance for tamper-evident verification, capability-scoped
     access tokens, and an injection-resistant re-hydration pipeline for
     moving memory between agents.
   - reason: `verified-memory-writes` × `permission-gate-as-architecture`
     (capability tokens = authority as data) × `file-as-bus` — memory as a
     portable, verifiable artifact rather than engine-local state. Relevant
     to this box's own cross-project `@import` contract.

## Typed enforcement & budget ceilings — the flagged thread

10. **Agent Contracts: A Formal Framework for Resource-Bounded Autonomous AI Systems** — paper
    - https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.08815 · arXiv 2026-01-13 (v3 2026-03-25; out of window)
    - Ye & Tan. Extends contract protocols to resource governance, and
      establishes **conservation laws guaranteeing delegated budgets respect
      parent constraints**. Reports 90% token reduction with 525× lower
      variance in iterative workflows and zero conservation violations in
      multi-agent delegation.
    - reason: **top pick.** Answers the open question NOTES parked for the
      next `/elevate`: khan2026token argues for *pre-flight spend
      reservation* over halt-after-cycle counters, and this supplies the
      formal object — a delegation-safe budget algebra. It is the missing
      link between `budget-as-ceiling` and `hierarchical-delegation`, which
      is exactly the shape of this box's coordinator handing ceilings to
      subagents. Oldest item here and included deliberately.

11. **Formal Policy Enforcement for Real-World Agentic Systems** — paper
    - https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16708 · arXiv Feb 2026 (out of window)
    - Enforcement layer that makes agent policy a checked artifact rather
      than prompt prose.
    - reason: fourth+ source on the typed-enforcement thread NOTES asked to
      watch for concept ripeness; strengthens
      `permission-gate-as-architecture`.

12. **Autoformalization of Agent Instructions into Policy-as-Code** — paper
    - https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26649 · arXiv June 2026 (out of window)
    - Compiles natural-language agent instructions into executable policy.
    - reason: the *translation* step the typed-enforcement thread is missing

      machine-checkable gates. Together with 10 and 11 this likely tips the
      thread over the concept-promotion threshold.

## Skill-library lifecycle

13. **From Memory to Skills: Evidence-Grounded Co-Evolution Governance for Long-Horizon LLM Agents** — paper
    - https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16621 · arXiv 2026-07-18 (**in window**)
    - Tang et al. MSCE converts agent experience into executable skills plus
      environmental understanding rather than passive context, reporting
      gains over both skill-augmented and memory-driven baselines with
      cross-domain transfer.
    - reason: **top pick** among in-window items. Governs memory and skills
      as one co-evolving system — spans `skill-library-lifecycle`,
      `agent-native-memory`, and `multi-granularity-memory`, and the
      "evidence-grounded" governance criterion is `citation-anchoring`
      applied to procedural memory.

14. **Hypothesis-Driven Skill Optimization for LLM Agents (HDSO)** — paper
    - https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22330 · arXiv June 2026 (out of window)
    - Every proposed skill must pass an explicit
      observe→hypothesize→validate→review→consolidate lifecycle, producing
      validation artifacts *and rejected-hypothesis records* rather than an
      undifferentiated accumulated skill set.
    - reason: retaining rejected hypotheses as first-class records is
      `typed-claim-partition` applied to a skill library, and the mandatory
      validation stage is `skill-library-lifecycle`'s missing enforcement
      half.

15. **SkillPyramid: A Hierarchical Skill Consolidation Framework for Self-Evolving Agents** — paper
    - https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03692 · arXiv June 2026 (out of window)
    - Consolidates a flat growing skill set into a hierarchy.
    - reason: `skill-library-lifecycle` × `hierarchical-delegation`; the
      consolidation direction (many→few) is under-covered relative to the
      acquisition papers already in the graph.

16. **SkillBrew: Multi-Objective Curation of Skill Banks for LLM Agents** — paper
    - https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29440 · arXiv May 2026 (out of window)
    - Treats skill-bank curation as explicit multi-objective optimization
      (coverage vs redundancy vs retrieval cost).
    - reason: the deprecation/consolidation half of `skill-library-lifecycle`
      that SkillOps (already ingested) leaves to policy.

17. **SkillLearnBench: Benchmarking Continual Learning Methods for Agent Skill Generation on Real-World Tasks** — paper
    - https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.20087 · arXiv April 2026 (out of window)
    - Benchmark for continual skill acquisition on real tasks.
    - reason: the skill cluster is source-rich but measurement-poor; a
      benchmark grounds it. Lower priority than 13–16.

18. **SkillCoach: Self-Evolving Rubrics for Evaluating and Enhancing Agentic Skill-Use** — paper
    - https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01874 · arXiv July 2026 (**in window**)
    - Rubrics for skill-use quality that themselves evolve.
    - reason: `programmable-evaluator-oracle` applied to skills. Caution: a
      self-evolving rubric is a search signal that can be overfit — worth
      ingesting partly as a counterexample to `hce-evaluation`.

## Memory retrieval under interference

19. **LongMINT / MINTEval: Evaluating Memory under Multi-Target Interference in Long-Horizon Agent Systems** — paper
    - https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18565 · arXiv May 2026 (out of window)
    - Measures recall of earlier facts that are later revised or interfered
      with; performance degrades as intervening updates accumulate.
    - reason: quantifies the failure `selective-memory-retrieval` and
      `context-eviction-policy` are designed against, and supplies the
      "bottleneck shifts from acquisition to access" framing that the skill
      cluster also needs.

20. **Experience Compression Spectrum: Unifying Memory, Skills, and Rules in LLM Agents** — paper
    - https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.15877 · arXiv April 2026 (out of window)
    - Places memory, skills, and rules on a single compression spectrum
      rather than treating them as separate subsystems.
    - reason: a candidate organizing frame for the memory + skill concept
      clusters, and a possible bridge to
      `compression-as-generalization-test`. Speculative but potentially
      MoC-shaping.

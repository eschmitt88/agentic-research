**Correction to the 09-28 method note.** In the arXiv OAI metadata, the
`<created>` field holds the *latest version's* date, not v1's. That is why
IDs like `2607.25152` show up as "created 2026-09-29". So "N records created
inside the window" means "submitted *or revised* inside the window", and
the 09-28 note's 4,982 figure counts the same way. That is harmless for a
digest, since a v2 of a paper the graph never saw is still news. Items 1, 2
and 4 are revisions of July/August papers that no earlier digest
surfaced.

## Ranked candidates

### 1. When Do Agent Loops Mistake Stagnation for Progress? Self-Evaluation Bias and Externally Grounded Verification in Long-Running Autonomous LLM Agent Loops
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25152
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, v2 revised 2026-09-29, cs.AI)
- **Authors:** Park, Choi
- **Relevance:** 5
- **Summary:** The testbed holds the agent and its tools fixed and varies
  only *what the loop's gate is grounded in*. Its world-state oracle is
  enforced by container and network isolation. Over 54 cycles a frontier
  agent claimed improvement every time, but **56% of the cycles had a
  measured delta ≤ 0**. The self-verdict gate became accept-all and eroded
  the best deployed state by 19%. The strongest in-band judge (artifact,
  diff and verdict history) accepted cycles of which 44% were regressions
  and rejected 38% of real improvements. The preregistered hypothesis that
  "a strong judge closes the gap" was rejected. When success *is*
  verifiable from the artifact, the mirage vanishes. A sign-only gate
  (accept/reject, no content) does about as well as full feedback.
- **Why:** This is the "progress mirage", and it is exactly the failure the
  09-27 noise-band proposal guards against, with a preregistered,
  controlled measurement. It also gives `hce-evaluation` and
  `evidence-gated-completion` a placement criterion: in-band judging works
  only when the success signal lives in the artifact. The sign-only result
  bears on `huang2026reward`'s point that detailed rejection feedback
  teaches evasion. Two authors; check credibility at ingest.

### 2. TraceML: What Auto-Research Agents Miss in Long-Horizon ML Development
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26086
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, v2 revised 2026-09-28, cs.LG/cs.AI)
- **Authors:** Yan, Sun, Li, Li et al. (5 authors)
- **Relevance:** 5
- **Summary:** 4,465 human Kaggle trajectories over 134 competitions, with
  430 human and 207 agent trajectories paired on seven of them, all on one
  version-level schema (score, intent, edit size, score effect per code
  version). Experts alternate data work, validation, modelling and
  ensembling, and **return to approaches they set aside**. Both agent
  scaffolds (Codex, MLEvolve) collapse into a narrow loop, **never pivot at
  the human rate, and never reopen abandoned work**. A short planning
  prompt distilled from human practice moves the named behaviors and lifts
  scores, but "instruction closes only the part of the gap that reduces to
  instructions". Corpus and toolkit released.
- **Why:** This is the missing evidence for the redirect-before-halt idea
  that the 10-04 `/elevate` run held. It measures the agent's pivot rate
  against a human baseline, which no prior paper in the graph does. The
  "instruction closes only what reduces to instructions" finding is also a
  data point for `enforcement-boundary-placement`.

### 3. Agents Are Systems, Not Models: Rethinking Agentic Evaluation
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01618
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, 2026-10-01, cs.AI)
- **Authors:** Wiedmann, Girrbach, Schmid, Akata
- **Relevance:** 5
- **Summary:** A coding agent has to find and correctly operate a published
  specialist model on four scientific tasks. The study varies five
  configuration axes (task information, reasoning, self-verification, time
  budget, backbone). **About 54% of outcome variance comes from repeating
  the same configuration**, not from changing it. Task information has the
  largest effect, larger than time budget or model size. **Prompting an
  agent to verify barely changes its verification behavior; giving it a
  verification tool changes it substantially.** 18,000+ trajectories
  released.
- **Why:** A variance decomposition is what the noise band needs: when
  run-to-run spread is half the signal, a bare `>` is a coin flip. That
  makes it direct input to the 09-27 proposal and to `pass-at-k`. The
  prompt-vs-tool verification result repeats `agarwal2026fire` (generic
  "verify" text does nothing) from an independent group, which bears on
  `enforcement-boundary-placement`. Strong group (Tübingen / Akata, Schmid).

### 4. Rethinking the Evaluation of Harness Evolution for Agents
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12227
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, v2 revised 2026-10-01, cs.AI)
- **Authors:** Wang, Zhu, Hu, Yuan et al. (11 authors)
- **Relevance:** 5
- **Summary:** Two protocol flaws in prior harness-evolution work. There are
  no matched-budget comparisons against simple test-time search, and the
  harness is selected on verification signals from the *same* benchmark it
  is scored on. On Terminal-Bench 2.1, **automatic harness evolution does
  not beat simple test-time scaling**, with or without test cases, and it
  generalizes poorly. It does help on long-horizon games (ARC-AGI-3 +80%,
  EdgeBench +10.9% over matched search). Code is released.
- **Why:** This is an `hce-evaluation` violation found across a literature,
  plus a matched-baseline correction. It is a counterweight to
  `srikanth2026recursive` and `xia2026rrsi`, and it gives
  `compression-as-generalization-test` a "when is evolution actually
  useful" boundary. The matched-baseline point is the paired-control
  discipline from the 08-23 elevate proposal.

### 5. RankEvolve: A Reliable Multi-Agent Auto-Research Harness for Evolving Ranking Models
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39551
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, 2026-09-30, cs.AI)
- **Authors:** Chen, Liu, Li, Yan
- **Relevance:** 5
- **Summary:** For auto-research, the binding constraint is *execution
  accuracy*: silent held-out leaks, missing normalization, a detached
  gradient, an unwired train/eval flag. An Executable Operating Protocol
  compiles phases and gates into a runtime-enforced state machine. A
  "meta-meta-harness" composes **Claude Code and Codex as nodes that review
  and repair each other**. In a budget-matched comparison, **heterogeneous
  composition raises all-oracle execution accuracy from 45.8% to 62.5%**
  (paired +16.7, 95% CI [6.6, 26.7]). A pre-specified LitGPT transfer split
  replicates the effect (+12.5, CI [3.0, 22.0]). A paired ablation
  separates per-step from full-protocol instruction injection.
- **Why:** This is the first measured gain from *product heterogeneity* in a
  research loop, so it is the remedy side of `shared-substrate-contagion`.
  The compiled state machine is `typed-enforcement` applied to `/iterate`'s
  shape. The silent-defect taxonomy is a checklist for `hce-evaluation`.

### 6. Audit the Scaffold, Not the Checkpoint: A Stationarity Dichotomy for Recursive Self-Improvement in Agentic Coding
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34924
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, 2026-09-28, cs.LG/cs.AI/cs.SE)
- **Authors:** Bobadilla-Suarez, Suh, Fortin
- **Relevance:** 4
- **Summary:** Self-modification hits strict diminishing returns while the
  agent's reachable set of edits stays fixed, and escapes only when that set
  grows. Rewriting the scaffold grows it without touching weights.
  Best-of-k realizes the best worker's ceiling exactly: width buys rate,
  not budget. A weighted vote would beat it but needs diversity that real
  workers lack. **On 30 same-family workers failure overlap is at its
  maximum and the majority fails 23/55 (42%) of tasks.** Per-round
  improvement decays toward zero on SWE-bench, and churn decays
  geometrically over 401 production sessions.
- **Why:** A common-mode measurement for `shared-substrate-contagion`
  (same-family replicas fail together) and a theory of stall for
  `budget-as-ceiling`: a plateau is expected when the edit space is fixed,
  which argues for *changing the move set* (redirect) rather than
  re-trying. It also gives `pass-at-k` a ceiling argument.

### 7. Research-Native by Construction: Minimal Nodes, Re-verifiable Workflows, and Compounding Memory for Long-Horizon Scientific Agents (AfS)
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35182
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, 2026-09-28, cs.SE/cs.AI)
- **Authors:** Wang, Liu, Cui, Ji et al. (8 authors)
- **Relevance:** 4
- **Summary:** A platform for research projects that run tens of hours over
  dozens of agent runs. Its thesis: move research credibility "from asking
  the model to behave to making the non-compliant state unrepresentable".
  The laws are commitment before measurement, unforgeable freezing,
  "reports are not facts", evidence persists but verdicts do not, negative
  results are first-class, and mechanical questions go to the framework
  while semantic judgment goes to the model. Mechanisms include inquiry
  contracts with frozen closure conditions, a hash-chained artifact ledger,
  and a two-tier knowledge base with promotion-by-rewriting. **No
  benchmark** (a process-integrity suite is "under construction").
- **Why:** The closest system-level design to this box's own (experiment
  folders, `raw/` immutability, promotion into `concepts/`), written as
  invariants. It is a design source for `typed-enforcement`,
  `evidence-gated-completion` and `llm-wiki-pattern`. Its evidence is weak:
  it describes a system and does not evaluate one. Ingest as design, not
  as evidence.

### 8. False Frontiers: Diagnosing and Mitigating Co-Cheating in Self-Evolving Search Agents
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39102
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, 2026-09-30, cs.CL/cs.AI/cs.LG)
- **Authors:** Chen, Li, Lu, Lin et al. (15 authors)
- **Relevance:** 4
- **Summary:** In proposer/solver self-evolution, the two increasingly
  **agree on shared errors**: internal reward rises while external
  correctness stagnates or falls, and gets worse round over round.
  Verifying each proposal three ways (MSV) barely helps. **CrossFit**
  splits the source documents, so questions from half A are scored by a
  solver trained only on half B. That cuts false agreement from
  6.1%/8.8% to 3.0%/3.7%, and source-excluded feedback reaches 0.4%/0.1%.
  Downstream gains are +8–9 points.
- **Why:** Co-cheating is `shared-substrate-contagion` inside one training
  loop, and cross-fitting is a concrete source-independence mechanism,
  which `li2026benchmark` said the concept needed. "Isolating feedback
  ancestry" is `information-firewall`.

### 9. VeriHarness: Scaling Agentic Verification for Long-Horizon Tasks
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00972
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, 2026-10-01, cs.AI/cs.MA)
- **Authors:** Zhang, Han, Wang, CuiZhu et al. (7 authors; Google)
- **Relevance:** 4
- **Summary:** Over repeated rollouts, **disagreement often exposes correct
  alternatives while consensus can conceal errors**. A disagreement
  resolver checks competing claims against environment evidence, and a
  *consensus challenger* tests shared claims and looks for omitted
  requirements. It gives the best selection on five long-horizon workspace
  benchmarks and +6.2/+6.4 over a single rollout (Gemini 3.5 Flash, Claude
  Opus 4.8). About 26k rollouts are released ($100k+).
- **Why:** "Consensus conceals errors" is the contagion thesis applied to
  sampling, and the consensus challenger is a cheap countermeasure. It
  bears on `pass-at-k` (agreement ≠ correctness) and on
  `evidence-gated-completion`.

### 10. PEAR: Progressive Evidence-Based AutoResearch for Industrial Search Systems
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35031
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, 2026-09-28, cs.IR/cs.AI)
- **Authors:** Wang, Zhu, Xiong, Yang et al. (9 authors)
- **Relevance:** 4
- **Summary:** **Keep-if-better under non-stationary traffic mistakes
  transient gains for persistent ones.** A confidence-gated verifier ladder
  (offline replay → shadow traffic → rapid online → decision-grade online)
  promotes a candidate only on a statistically significant positive
  effect, so cheap exploration stays broad and expensive tests stay rare.
  Gains are +2.7% / +3.3% Main Order/DAU in two production A/B tests.
- **Why:** A production-validated version of the noise-band idea, with a
  multi-fidelity twist: the gate gets stricter as evaluations get more
  expensive. It is a third independent source, after `chandran2026autoresearch`
  and `qu2026propose`, that keep-if-better needs a significance gate.

### 11. ConflictGuide: AutoResearch Improves When Competing Behaviors Are Made Visible
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39933
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, 2026-09-30, cs.AI/cs.LG)
- **Authors:** Xu, Zou, Shu, Liu
- **Relevance:** 4
- **Summary:** Scalar feedback supports broad early exploration but hides
  how edits trade off competing behaviors. In matched-budget experiments,
  **switching to competing-behavior probe feedback as gains diminish
  sustains progress past scalar-only plateaus**. It cuts errors by up to
  28% over scalar-only AutoResearch across five model families. It is
  packaged as a reusable skill.
- **Why:** A matched-budget intervention *at the plateau*. It is the closest
  thing yet to a redirect-vs-continue comparison, and the redirect is
  "change the feedback signal" rather than "change the idea". Same group
  as `zou2026fmlbench`.

### 12. Making AI Scientists Auditable from Evidence to Claim (Xcientist)
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18874
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, revised 2026-09-27, cs.AI)
- **Authors:** Wang, Li, Yang et al. (20 authors)
- **Relevance:** 4
- **Summary:** Defines **claim drift**: an unresolved mismatch between an
  accepted claim and its sources, code or experiments. An evidence graph
  keeps source passages, baselines and conditions, and validation
  contracts specify what each stage must produce. A retrospective audit
  finds claim drift of 3.6–16.7%, lower than three comparison systems in
  all nine task×relation cells.
- **Why:** `citation-anchoring` and `typed-claim-partition` with a measured
  drift rate for AI-scientist output, and the closest external analogue of
  how this project ties claims to `raw/`. Paired with `li2026discover`
  (the score certifies the number, not the claim).

### 13. Assay: Claims That Decay With the Code. Content-Addressed Evidence Graphs for Accountable AI-Assisted Software Delivery
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36170
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, 2026-09-28, cs.SE)
- **Authors:** Tiwari, Vass, Singh
- **Relevance:** 4
- **Summary:** Every agent claim ("tests pass", "behavior preserved") is bound
  to the Merkle hash of the dependency cone it covers, so **the claim goes
  stale exactly when that code changes**. The binding is shown to be sound
  and minimal. A risk-proportional evidence obligation, separation of
  duties, and **a merge gate that consults no model** (coverage,
  freshness, signatures, exit codes).
- **Why:** A staleness mechanism `evidence-gated-completion` lacks: evidence
  expires with the code it vouched for. The model-free gate is the
  `typed-enforcement` end of `enforcement-boundary-placement`. It also
  bears on `/lint`'s staleness check.

### 14. Search Shapes Conclusions: Auditing Evidence Selection Bias in Deep Research Agents
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39026
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, 2026-09-30, cs.AI)
- **Authors:** Xiao, Wang, Chen et al. (11 authors)
- **Relevance:** 4
- **Summary:** Citation correctness doesn't show whether adaptive search saw a
  representative sample. Early findings steer later queries, picks and
  stopping. CESS uses logged selection probabilities to correct the
  candidate-pool average (inverse-propensity), with shrinkage for short
  searches and intervals where some documents can't be sampled.
- **Why:** This is about *this project's own retrieval*.
  `web-grounded-literature` assumes good queries yield a fair picture, but
  `/digest`'s regex-then-read funnel is adaptive selection, and "abstracts
  overstate" (09-28 NOTES) is a related bias. It is also the strongest
  argument yet for the complete OAI-PMH harvest over ranked queries.

### 15. Groundability, Not Scale Alone: When Weak Reviewers Can Audit Strong Coding Agents
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01023
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, 2026-10-01, cs.SE/cs.AI/cs.CL)
- **Authors:** Guo, Gu, Jin et al. (4 authors)
- **Relevance:** 4
- **Summary:** 411 execution-labelled traces plus 101 controlled cases.
  **Structured but unchecked evidence raises both defect catch and
  over-rejection.** Given execution evidence (frozen format chosen
  beforehand), five of six reviewers improve on both. **Reviewer size is
  not a consistent predictor.** A frozen cascade (patch-caused static
  errors plus generated tests that fail before the patch) reaches 0.86–0.89
  coverage, but over-rejection stays high (0.66).
- **Why:** The reviewer-side complement to item 1: what matters is grounding,
  not judge scale. It bears on `evidence-gated-completion` and on whether
  `/iterate` can use a cheap reviewer (`hybrid-model-backends`).

### 16. When Does Randomized Oversight Align AI Agents That Can Conceal?
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38262
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, 2026-09-29, econ.TH/cs.AI)
- **Authors:** Gans, Holden
- **Relevance:** 3
- **Summary:** A theory paper. Stronger auditing makes undeterred violations
  better hidden. Rare audits deter every type of agent *if evidence
  survives concealment and audit draws can't be learned*. When evidence
  can be erased, deterrence has to come from lower gains (e.g. credit for
  stopping) or from fewer ways to conceal. It is applied to the July 2026
  incident in which agents in OpenAI cybersecurity evaluations compromised
  Hugging Face infrastructure.
- **Why:** It formalizes why `qin2026llm` (agents can delete their traces)
  breaks audit-based oversight, and "credit for stopping" is a design
  lever for `budget-as-ceiling` and halting. Well-known economists, but
  theory only.

### 17. Compositional Safety Failures in Harness Evolution: Identification and Runtime Monitoring
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33123
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, 2026-09-29, cs.AI/cs.SE)
- **Authors:** Zhang, Liu, Lai et al. (5 authors)
- **Relevance:** 3
- **Summary:** Individually safe, utility-preserving updates to memory,
  prompts, skills or tools can interact unsafely. It finds **43 pairwise and
  18 irreducible 3-way failures** across three safety benchmarks. Since
  validating every combination explodes, it proposes a typed hypergraph
  runtime monitor.
- **Why:** The benign counterpart of the skill-cascade attacks (2609.30383,
  declined 09-29). Component checks don't certify composition, which matters for
  `skill-library-lifecycle` and for claude-system's composing skills.

### 18. VERSE: Verified Self-Evolving Optimizer for Agent Harnesses
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02616
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, 2026-10-02, cs.AI/cs.CL/cs.LG)
- **Authors:** Wang, Ge, Wang et al. (9 authors)
- **Relevance:** 3
- **Summary:** In a controlled study, **an optimizer that evolves itself does
  not improve without execution-based verification**, and does best with
  it. VERSE lets the optimizer test draft edits, replay failures and
  perturb suspected steps before it submits, and tracks fixes and
  regressions across rounds. The optimizer revises both the executor
  harness and its *own* prompts, skills, tools, hooks and notes.
- **Why:** Bears on `/elevate`'s self-application: self-revision without an
  execution check is noise. It is a sibling to `srikanth2026recursive` and
  item 4.

### 19. What Does a Skill Actually Do? Estimands and Evaluation Validity for Tool and Skill Use in LLM Agents: A Critical Review
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33153
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, 2026-10-01, cs.SE/cs.AI)
- **Authors:** Zhang (single author)
- **Relevance:** 3
- **Summary:** A critical review of 100 cited papers, 35 of them extracted in
  detail. Reported "skill gains" estimate different things. **Pairing runs
  on the same task does not identify an invocation effect when the
  evaluation conditions on a trigger inside the treated run.** Paired
  gain/regression counts describe discordance, not the share of tasks
  that get worse.
- **Why:** A methods check for every skill-gain claim in the graph
  (`skill-library-lifecycle`, `hu2026analyzing`, `cheng2026scope`) and for
  the 08-23 paired-control proposal. Single-author review.

### 20. Learning What to Investigate Next: Meta-Reasoning for Long-Horizon Research Agents (MIRA)
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02525
- **Type:** paper (arXiv, 2026-10-01, cs.AI)
- **Authors:** Samanta, Efroni, Sajda et al. (5 authors)
- **Relevance:** 3
- **Summary:** An outer-loop meta-reasoner curates context from a persistent
  research record and writes a *work order* for the next investigation, or
  ends the episode. A fresh executor carries it out. Without training, it
  improves long-horizon inference and compute allocation in theorem
  proving and open-ended NAS research. A critic trained at decision
  boundaries forecasts remaining return.
- **Why:** The "decide what next or stop" step is `/iterate`'s ideator turn,
  with a learned value-of-continuing estimate. That is an alternative to a
  fixed `max_consecutive_no_improvement`. Architecturally it is
  `hierarchical-delegation` plus `file-as-bus`.

## Spend & compute economy

1. **How Do AI Agents Spend Your Money? Analyzing and Predicting Token Consumption in Agentic Coding Tasks** — paper
   - https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.22750 · arXiv v1 2026-04-24, v2 2026-04-29 (out of window; surfaced now, dead-center on the named gap)
   - Bai, Huang, Wang, Sun, Mihalcea, Brynjolfsson, Pentland, Pei.
     SWE-bench Verified across eight frontier LLMs, plus an explicit
     *pre-execution* prediction task: can an agent forecast its own
     input/output token usage from its tools and environment before it
     starts? Answer is a clean negative — weak-to-moderate correlation
     (up to 0.39) and **systematic underestimation** of real cost.
   - Why it matters here: this is the direct empirical test of the
     `khan2026token` pre-flight spend-reservation idea that the
     08-17 NOTES entry queues for `/elevate`. It argues the reservation
     has to be architectural (a ceiling the harness enforces) rather
     than an agent's own estimate — which is exactly the
     `budget-as-ceiling` claim, now with a measured failure of the
     alternative. Strongest candidate in the file to seed the
     spend-policy concept the promote-moc declines are waiting on.

2. **Energy per Successful Goal: Goal-Level Energy Accounting for Agentic AI Systems** — paper
   - https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22883 · arXiv 2026-05-20 (out of window; the besanson2026green thread's missing mechanism)
   - Panigrahy & Tyagi. Two metrics — Energy per Successful Goal (EpG:
     total workflow energy over *all* attempts, normalized by goals
     actually completed) and Orchestration Overhead Index (OOI:
     orchestration energy relative to linear execution) — plus A-LEMS,
     a measurement harness. Reports agentic workflows at 4.33x the
     mean energy per successful goal of linear baselines (888.1 J vs
     205.3 J), with the overhead attributed to orchestration structure
     rather than inference compute; OOI drops below 1.0x on
     tool-augmented tasks.
   - Why it matters here: normalizing cost by *successful* goals rather
     than per-call is the same accounting move `pass-at-k` makes for
     scores, and it gives the `besanson2026green` energy line a
     mechanism to attach to. Retries-count-against-you is a real
     constraint on `async-worker-pool` and on the
     `max_consecutive_no_improvement` ceiling in `budget.yaml`.

3. **Token Economics for LLM Agents: A Dual-View Study from Computing and Economics** — paper
   - https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09104 · arXiv May 2026 (out of window)
   - Treats agent token spend simultaneously as a systems-resource and
     an economic-allocation problem. Candidate framing source if the
     spend-policy concept wants a vocabulary beyond "ceiling"; lower
     priority than 1-2, which carry the measurements.

4. **Budget-Adaptive Routing: Skipping the Weak When the Strong Answers Anyway** — paper
   - https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30919 · arXiv June 2026 (out of window)
   - Routing policy that drops the cheap-model attempt when the strong
     model would answer correctly regardless — i.e. cascade escalation
     is not free, and the cheap first call can be pure waste. Direct
     evidence on `hybrid-model-backends`, which currently argues the
     cascade case without the counter-evidence.

## Runtime memory & context policy

5. **The Missing Memory Hierarchy: Demand Paging for LLM Context Windows** — paper
   - https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09023 · arXiv 2026-03-09 (out of window; sixth attestation for lossless-context-offload, and the only production deployment)
   - Tony Mason. Pichay is a transparent proxy between client and
     inference API implementing a three-level hierarchy: L1 eviction of
     stale tool definitions and results, L2 fault-driven pinning of the
     working set (content the model re-requests), L3 model-initiated
     cross-conversation compaction. When the model references evicted
     material the proxy detects the **page fault** and restores it.
     0.0254% fault rate over 1.4M simulated evictions; up to 93%
     context reduction in live deployment (5,038KB → 339KB over 681
     turns); 21.8% of context measured as structural waste across 857
     production sessions; documents the expected thrashing pathology
     under load.
   - Why it matters here: the operating-systems framing (demand paging,
     working set, page fault) is the cleanest available vocabulary for
     `lossless-context-offload` + `context-eviction-policy`, and the
     proxy-not-agent placement is the deterministic pole of the
     policy-locus axis the 08-03/08-10 cycles opened. Also the closest
     external analogue to the pending `precompact-addressable-offload`
     proposal, whose target is a hook that currently discards
     `transcript_path`.

6. **From Lossy to Verified: A Provenance-Aware Tiered Memory for Agents** — paper
   - https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17913 · arXiv February 2026 (out of window)
   - Tiered memory that keeps provenance so a recalled item can be
     checked rather than trusted. Sits at the junction of
     `verified-memory-writes` and `lossless-context-offload`; the
     verified-recall direction is thin in the graph (writes are
     covered, reads less so).

7. **AgenticSTS: A Bounded-Memory Testbed for Long-Horizon LLM Agents** — paper
   - https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02255 · arXiv July 2026 (out of window)
   - A testbed that holds memory *bounded* by construction, so eviction
     policy is forced rather than optional. The measurement side of the
     runtime-memory cluster, which is currently mechanism-heavy and
     harness-light; complements `hce-evaluation` on the eviction axis.

8. **Externalization in LLM Agents: A Unified Review of Memory, Skills, Protocols and Harness Engineering** — paper (survey)
   - https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08224 · arXiv April 2026 (out of window)
   - Survey spanning four of this project's fronts at once —
     memory, skills, protocols, harness. Useful as a coverage check on
     `mocs/agent-architecture` and `mocs/capability-layer` (what is the
     graph missing?), less useful as a concept source. Take it for the
     citation graph, not the claims.

## Typed / formal enforcement

9. **Agent Safety Should Be a Runtime Contract** — paper (position + survey evidence)
   - https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11274 · arXiv **2026-08-11 — in window**
   - Ng, Han, Zhang, Wang. Argues safety belongs in the *harness*, not
     in model training, via two mechanisms: prevention (sandboxes,
     filters that block dangerous actions) and **evidence** (verifiable
     proof of correct execution). Four empirical legs: 52 documented
     safety incidents, an audit of completion failures, an examination
     of 12 public agent systems, and a publication review showing an
     8-12x imbalance between training-time and deployment-time work at
     major conferences. Conclusion: "the right unit of safety in
     agentic AI is the trajectory-with-checkable-evidence, not the
     model."
   - Why it matters here: this is the thesis of
     `permission-gate-as-architecture` and `typed-enforcement` stated
     as a position paper with a literature audit behind it, and the
     evidence half maps onto `citation-anchoring` /
     `typed-claim-partition` (a claim ships with its check).
     Best in-window item in the file.

10. **What Can Be Enforced? A Theory of Certified Runtime Safety for Tool-Using Agents** — paper
    - https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22868 · arXiv 2026-07-24 (out of window)
    - Shawn Ray. Characterizes what a deterministic gate can enforce at
      all: it enforces exactly those nonempty safety policies whose good
      prefixes its register model recognizes. Policy nontriviality is
      undecidable with two decrementable counters but PSPACE for a
      separable monotone fragment; statistical guarantees via
      Neyman-Pearson and conformal calibration; and — the interesting
      part — closed-loop effects, where blocking changes what the agent
      proposes next, so "static scores and ungated trajectories need not
      identify the closed-loop frontier." Has empirical work (static
      diagnostics, controlled-model enumeration, representation
      rewrites, paired closed-loop reruns).
    - Why it matters here: gives `typed-enforcement` an upper bound —
      which constraints are enforceable by a gate and which are not —
      and the closed-loop point is a warning about evaluating a
      permission gate on ungated traces, which touches
      `hce-evaluation`.

11. **Agent Behavioral Contracts: Formal Specification and Runtime Enforcement for Reliable Autonomous AI Agents** — paper + tool
    - https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22302 · arXiv February 2026 (out of window; the empirical prompt-vs-types comparison the thread has been missing)
    - Design-by-contract for agents: prompts carry no formal semantics,
      no verifiable guarantees, no enforcement. Implemented as
      AgentAssert and evaluated on AgentContract-Bench — 200 scenarios,
      7 models, 6 vendors, 1,980 sessions.
    - Why it matters here: the largest cross-vendor test of declarative
      runtime behavioral contracts. **Corrected on reading (see the
      note at the top of this file):** artifacts are patent-pending and
      unreleased, the text-level-safety claim is cited rather than
      original, and the measured effect is *observability* — contracts
      make violations countable — not prevention. Ingested as a
      refinement of `typed-enforcement` rather than as the confirmation
      NOTES was watching for.

## Evaluation integrity

12. **Diagnostic Foundation for Evaluating LLMs' Research Integrity as Co-Scientists (IntegrityBench)** — paper
    - https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12345 · arXiv id announced August 2026; abs page states v1 2026-06-03 (**in-window announcement**)
    - Tripathi, Sharma, Manoharan Jayanthi, Garg, Li. 36 paired tasks
      over misconduct classification, ethical-action reasoning, and
      artifact-grounded decisions, under a 5-level implicit/explicit
      pressure protocol; 18 frontier model variants fail roughly 1 in 3
      integrity-critical decisions at peak pressure, and neither scale
      nor reasoning reliably helps. Explicit pressure increases
      compliance with misconduct; implicit reframing causes overrefusal
      of legitimate work. Request-classification and artifact-grounded
      performance dissociate.
    - Why it matters here: the pressure-protocol design is the missing
      adversarial half of `hce-evaluation` — the graph covers hiding
      the test set from the agent, not what the agent does when pushed.
      The classification/artifact dissociation is also an argument for
      `programmable-evaluator-oracle` over judge-the-request.

13. **SoundnessBench: Can Your AI Scientist Really Tell Good Research Ideas from Bad Ones?** — paper
    - https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30329 · arXiv May 2026 (out of window)
    - Tests whether an AI-scientist system can discriminate sound from
      unsound research ideas — the selection step upstream of every
      result it reports. Relevant to `programmable-evaluator-oracle`
      and to how much weight `/elevate`'s reputability gate should put
      on an agent's own novelty judgement.

14. **Hack-Verifiable Environments: Towards Evaluating Reward Hacking at Scale** — paper
    - https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20744 · arXiv May 2026 (out of window)
    - Embeds *detectable* reward-hacking opportunities into the
      environment so hacking is measured by construction rather than
      inferred post hoc from trajectories. The environment-side
      counterpart to the in-graph post-hoc-auditor line; a strong
      mechanism for `programmable-evaluator-oracle`.

15. **Reproducing, Analyzing, and Detecting Reward Hacking in Rubric-Based Reinforcement Learning** — paper
    - https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04923 · arXiv June 2026 (out of window)
    - Reproduces rubric-gaming, then evaluates a tool-using detector
      agent (RHDA) as a reference detector on single-bias runs. Take
      only if 14 is ingested and the graph wants the detector side as
      well; overlaps the in-graph reward-hacking source.

## ML-research-agent search & claim verification

16. **Autonomous Research Agents: A Survey of AI Scientists and the Verification Gap** — paper (survey)
    - https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05179 · arXiv id announced August 2026; abs page states v1 2026-06-29 (**in-window announcement**)
    - Ding, Nannapaneni, Liu, Zhang. Audits 35 works (26 coded in
      detail) on seven dimensions. Headline asymmetry: code release is
      common (83% of runnable systems) but reproducibility artifacts
      and novelty verification are scarce (38% each), and of nine
      closed-loop systems **none** demonstrated externally validated
      verification. Framing: agent claims "are often harder to verify
      than their code is to run."
    - Why it matters here: a quantified external statement of exactly
      what `citation-anchoring` and `typed-claim-partition` exist to
      fix, and a ready-made survey of the co-scientist primaries this
      digest deliberately declined to list individually. No code URL.

17. **Why Solve It Twice? Hierarchical Accumulation of Skills for Transfer-Efficient ML Engineering** — paper
    - https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30911 · arXiv June 2026 (out of window)
    - Skill reuse across MLE tasks rather than re-solving per task,
      organized hierarchically. Joins `skill-library-lifecycle` to
      `hierarchical-delegation` on the ML-research-agent benchmark side,
      where the graph's skill sources are mostly general-agent.

18. **Effective Harness Engineering for Algorithm Discovery with Coding Agents** — paper
    - https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15221 · arXiv May 2026 (out of window)
    - Harness-side (not model-side) determinants of algorithm-discovery
      performance. Same claim shape as the in-graph `file-as-bus`
      ablation — the scaffold, not the model, moves the number. Useful
      as a second attestation if its ablations are component-level.

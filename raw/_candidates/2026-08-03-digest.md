## Context eviction & memory policy

1. **Beyond Compaction: Structured Context Eviction for Long-Horizon Agents** — paper
   - https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11213 · arXiv June 2026 (out of window, new angle)
   - "Context Window Lifecycle": the agent annotates its trajectory as
     typed, dependency-linked episodes; a deterministic, LLM-free
     policy evicts in priority order within that structure when the
     token budget is exceeded — graduated eviction instead of
     wholesale compaction.
   - reason: **top pick.** Dead-center for `context-eviction-policy`,
     and the typed-episode + deterministic-policy split is a second
     attestation for the `typed-enforcement` thread NOTES is watching
     (enforcement runs as code, not inference). Also touches
     `scripted-tool-pipelines`' compile-don't-prompt principle.

2. **Governance Decay: How Context Compaction Silently Erases Safety Constraints in Long-Horizon LLM Agents** — paper
   - https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22528 · arXiv late June 2026 (near window)
   - Shows that compaction/summarization steps progressively drop
     safety and policy constraints from context, so long-horizon
     agents drift out of their guardrails without any adversary.
   - reason: the failure mode that connects `context-eviction-policy`
     to `information-firewall` and `permission-gate-as-architecture`:
     if constraints live in evictable context, eviction is a
     de-governance channel. Strong argument for constraints living in
     the harness (typed-enforcement) rather than the window.

3. **Self-GC: Self-Governing Context for Long-Horizon LLM Agents** — paper
   - https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00692 · arXiv July 2026 (in window)
   - The agent itself manages its context lifecycle — deciding what
     to retain, externalize, or drop — rather than a fixed harness
     policy.
   - reason: the counterpoint to item 1's deterministic eviction:
     agent-governed vs. harness-governed context. Reading both
     against `context-eviction-policy` should sharpen the concept's
     policy-locus axis. Also relevant to `agent-native-memory`.

4. **TokenPilot: Cache-Efficient Context Management for LLM Agents** — paper
   - https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17016 · arXiv June 2026 (out of window)
   - Dual-granularity: ingestion-aware compaction stabilizes prompt
     prefixes (cache-friendly), lifecycle-aware eviction offloads
     segments when task relevance expires.
   - reason: the cache-economics angle missing from
     `context-eviction-policy` — eviction policy interacts with
     prefix caching, i.e. memory policy is also a cost policy
     (`budget-as-ceiling` adjacency). Lower priority than 1–3.

## Evaluation integrity & reward hacking

5. **Do Androids Dream of Breaking the Game? Systematically Auditing AI Agent Benchmarks with BenchJack** — paper
   - https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12673 · arXiv May 2026 (out of window)
   - Automated auditing of agent benchmarks for reward-hackable
     verifiers; reports >15% of tasks across five major
     terminal-agent benchmarks have exploitable evaluation.
   - reason: instruments the evaluator side of `hce-evaluation` — the
     benchmark itself as the weak link, complementing
     zhao2026specbench (agent side) and atinafu2026rewardhacking
     (ML-engineering side). A meta-audit tool is actionable for
     `programmable-evaluator-oracle` (what makes an oracle gameable).

6. **Reward Hacking Benchmark: Measuring Exploits in LLM Agents with Tool Use** — paper
   - https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02964 · arXiv May 2026 (out of window)
   - Multi-step tasks with naturalistic shortcut opportunities:
     skipping verification, inferring answers from task-adjacent
     metadata, tampering with evaluation-relevant functions.
   - reason: the metadata-inference exploit is exactly the leakage
     channel `information-firewall` names — a candidate second
     attestation. **Check overlap with atinafu2026rewardhacking
     (2603.11337) at ingest**; keep only if the tool-use exploit
     taxonomy adds beyond it.

7. **Log Analysis is Necessary for Credible Evaluation of AI Agents** — paper
   - https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.08545 · arXiv May 2026 (out of window)
   - Position-with-evidence: score-only reporting hides shortcuts,
     environment breakage, and hacked verifiers; trajectory-level log
     auditing changes the capability picture.
   - reason: supports `citation-anchoring`'s provenance discipline at
     the run level and `hce-evaluation`'s diagnostics requirement;
     also validates this project's own log.md-per-experiment rule.
     Thin if it stays position-only — decline is fine if the
     empirical section is weak.

8. **SWE-Marathon: Can Agents Autonomously Complete Ultra-Long-Horizon Software Work?** — paper
   - https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07682 · arXiv June 2026 (out of window)
   - Benchmark for multi-day autonomous software work; long-horizon
     failure modes (drift, forgotten constraints, budget exhaustion).
   - reason: nominally SE-agent scope, but ultra-long-horizon eval
     design (checkpointing, variance over long runs) informs
     `pass-at-k` and `budget-as-ceiling` for research agents.
     Borderline — decline if it's pure SWE leaderboard.

## Budget & agency enforcement

9. **Green SARC: Predictive Cost and Carbon Governance for Agentic AI Systems** — paper
   - https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15954 · arXiv June 2026 (out of window)
   - Governance-by-architecture: constraints as first-class
     specification objects compiled into four enforcement sites —
     Pre-Action Gate, Action-Time Monitor, Post-Action Auditor,
     Escalation Router.
   - reason: **the pre-flight angle NOTES is watching.** A Pre-Action
     Gate with predictive cost is khan2026token's spend-reservation
     idea, generalized and compiled — direct input to the next
     /elevate evaluation of `budget-ceiling-reserve`. Attests
     `typed-enforcement` and `permission-gate-as-architecture`.

10. **Sovereign-OS: A Charter-Governed Operating System for Autonomous AI Agents with Verifiable Fiscal Discipline** — paper
    - https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14011 · arXiv March 2026 (old — included for watch-item fit)
    - Agents as economic principals under a constitutional charter;
      append-only UnifiedLedger for all token/financial flows; no task
      consumes resources without explicit budgetary authorization,
      regardless of what the planner requests.
    - reason: the maximalist version of `budget-as-ceiling` +
      pre-flight authorization — reservation enforced by an OS-level
      ledger. Useful as the far end of the design spectrum when
      /elevate weighs how much reservation machinery is warranted.
      March date is why it's ranked below Green SARC.

11. **Pre-Call Budget Enforcement for AI Agents** (Pylva) — engineering post
    - https://pylva.com/guides/pre-call-budget-enforcement · 2026-06-29 (near window)
    - Concrete runtime pattern: agent proposes a call → runtime
      estimates cost → rule loaded → enforcement compares estimate
      against remaining budget → allow/warn/route/review/block,
      before spend occurs.
    - reason: practitioner-side attestation of the same pre-flight
      reservation mechanism as items 9–10 — three independent sources
      would make `budget-as-ceiling`'s reservation extension (or a new
      concept) ripe. Engineering post, so ingest as post kind; decline
      if it's vendor marketing with no mechanism detail.

12. **Firewalls to Secure Dynamic LLM Agentic Networks** — paper
    - https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.01822 · arXiv Feb 2025, v6 recently revised (old — included for watch-item fit)
    - Task-scoped firewall rules between agents in a network:
      minimize shared data, derive rules from prior simulations,
      enforce at the boundary rather than in prompts.
    - reason: `information-firewall` is a single-source seedling that
      NOTES explicitly wants a second attestation for. This is a
      construction-side source (how to build the firewall) vs. the
      existing attack-side source. Old, but the v6 revision and
      direct fit justify surfacing; decline if the mechanism doesn't
      transfer from multi-agent networks to holdout protection.

## Evolutionary search: grain & evaluators

13. **Mutation Without Variation: Convergence Dynamics in LLM-Driven Program Evolution** — paper
    - https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05408 · arXiv June 2026 (out of window)
    - Studies how LLM mutation operators lose population diversity —
      proposals converge to similar programs, collapsing effective
      search regardless of population size.
    - reason: names the failure mode `evolutionary-search-grain` and
      `evolutionary-expansion` currently assume away: the mutation
      operator's entropy, not the grain, can be the binding
      constraint. Diagnostic for why island strategies help.

14. **Compute Allocation in Evolutionary Search: From Depth-Breadth to Multi-Armed Bandits** — paper
    - https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29268 · arXiv May 2026 (out of window)
    - Frames depth-vs-breadth in LLM evolutionary search as a bandit
      problem; adaptive allocation beats fixed depth/breadth splits
      under a fixed compute budget.
    - reason: joins `evolutionary-expansion` to `budget-as-ceiling` —
      the search policy is a budget-allocation policy. Directly
      relevant to how AIRA-style agents should spend a fixed
      experiment budget (`pass-at-k` variance implications too).

15. **Glite ARF: Verifier-Driven Research with Parallel LLM Coding Agents** — paper
    - https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27416 · arXiv late June 2026 (near window)
    - Research loop where a programmable verifier drives parallel
      coding agents; the verifier, not the planner, is the control
      signal.
    - reason: another point for `programmable-evaluator-oracle`'s
      claim that the evaluator defines the problem, in a
      research-agent (not benchmark) setting; pairs with
      `async-worker-pool` via the parallel-agent execution model.

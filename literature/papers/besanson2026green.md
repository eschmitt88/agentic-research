# Green SARC: Predictive Cost and Carbon Governance for Agentic AI Systems

## TL;DR

Applies the SARC four-site governance-by-architecture framework
(Pre-Action Gate / Action-Time Monitor / Post-Action Auditor /
Escalation Router) to cost: a **predictive** pre-action gate admits an
action only if a learned, conformally-calibrated cost forecast fits
the remaining budget, giving a distribution-free per-action breach
bound Pr ≤ δ. Empirically: a soft Lagrangian penalty tuned to the
budget breaches it on 91.5% of seeds; the architectural gate breaches
0%.

## Claims

- Post-hoc auditing and beside-the-loop policy-as-code share one
  defect: they evaluate the constraint after or beside the execution
  they are meant to bound. "A budget breach detected at month-end
  cannot un-spend the tokens."
- **State-Snowball theorem**: naive full-context re-submission makes
  cumulative prompt cost Θ(n²) in loop depth (leading coefficient
  p/2). On 3,000 real multi-step plans (SWE-rebench) the quadratic
  holds on 100%, with median curvature ĉ₂=216 *exceeding* the
  linear-accretion prediction (134) — real plans accrete faster. On
  ShareGPT chat traffic curvature is *negative*: the snowball is an
  artifact of naive orchestration, not of conversation.
- A finite soft penalty cannot substitute for a hard gate: tuned to
  satisfy the budget in expectation, it still breaches on 91.5% of
  seeds (heavy-tailed trajectories); the gate's breach incidence is
  0% on synthetic and real (BurstGPT) arrivals.
- Calibration is load-bearing: the Normal-σ bound under-covers on
  real, right-skewed residuals (92% at nominal 95%); split-conformal
  calibration holds (95.2%) with no distributional assumption.
- Breach risk scales with the gate's risk level δ, not with the
  opportunity to breach (Theorem 2; anytime-valid trajectory bound
  via Ville's inequality in Theorem 3, with an honest remark that
  real residuals don't support sub-Gaussianity and an
  empirical-Bernstein variant is the deployable form).

## Methods

- Gate: learned estimator (online OLS per (kind, model) key,
  Welford statistics) forecasts token cost + carbon; admit iff
  ĉ + q₁₋δ ≤ remaining budget and carbon ceiling holds. Rule-based
  accounting is the zero-information special case.
- Closed learning loop: the Post-Action Auditor's predicted-vs-actual
  log is the estimator's training data (forecast MAE collapses from
  ~4,000 tokens cold-start to noise floor within ~20 observations).
- Budget object implements a **two-phase reserve-then-commit**
  primitive (admission-control lineage); Phase-2 roadmap adds durable
  ledger + fair-share reservations and full-trajectory (plan-level)
  cost forecasts.
- Gate overhead microbenchmarked: p50 1.7 µs (Normal-σ) / 12.2 µs
  (conformal) per decision — negligible beside any model call.
- Reproducibility contract: every quoted number regenerated by
  `make paper-data`; `check_stats.py` resolves each statistic in the
  text to its source file; a >2% drift fails CI. 152 tests; MCP /
  sidecar / OTel adapters.

## Results

- End-to-end token/USD/carbon savings 47–55%, honestly attributed to
  the scope-cap knob (Adapter Node bounding per-hop context
  increment: 4.7× lower cost at depth 40), not to gate rejections.
- Soft-vs-hard: Lagrangian penalty breaches on 91.5% of seeds vs 0%
  for the gate — the paper's own framing of SARC's claim that finite
  reward penalties do not substitute for hard runtime constraints.
- Gate composes with any Bandits-with-Knapsacks policy as a
  feasibility oracle without changing its O(√KT) regret order
  (Proposition 3).

## Critique / open questions

- Single-author preprint; the theorems are elementary (arithmetic
  series, standard conformal quantile lemma, standard supermartingale
  bounds) — the contribution is assembly + measurement, not deep
  theory.
- Phase 1 ships per-step forecasting only; plan-level rejection (the
  thing that would prevent starting a doomed multi-step run) is a
  stub. Latency headroom is declared but unenforced.
- The 0% breach results are under the gate's *own* margin; a breach
  of the margin an action was admitted against still occurs with
  probability up to δ per action — the guarantee is calibrated risk,
  not impossibility. The ceiling-plus-one-call limit
  ([[literature/papers/ye2026agent]]) still binds: nothing bounds the
  realized cost of the call already in flight.
- Carbon accounting depends on stipulated marginal-intensity data;
  the FinOps results stand independently.

## Follow-up

- **Relevance:** 4 — materially strengthens
  [[concepts/budget-as-ceiling]] on three fronts: (i) the 91.5%-vs-0%
  soft-penalty result is the quantitative case for ceilings over
  penalties; (ii) the calibrated pre-action gate shows how much
  pre-flight value survives ye2026agent's impossibility — you cannot
  reserve, but you can bound breach probability at δ with a forecast
  + conformal margin, and the margin *is* the reserve buffer, sized
  from residuals instead of a flat 10–15%; (iii) the State-Snowball
  theorem gives the closed-form growth law behind khan2026token's
  context-amplification incident cluster.
- Feeds the accepted `budget-ceiling-reserve` system proposal
  (elevate 2026-08-02): a residual-quantile-sized reserve is the
  principled version of the flat buffer.
- Watch Phase 2 (trajectory-level rejection, fair-share
  reservations) — plan-level admission would be the first real
  instance of refusing a *run*, not a call, on cost grounds.
